## Who and what

**RelievApp is a web app of two-minute, hands-on sessions that help you move from a hard feeling to a more manageable one.** You feel anxious, angry, overthinking or low. You say how strong it is, you play a short session with one hand and your eyes open, and you say how you feel after. It runs in the browser on a phone or a computer, in English and Spanish, with nothing to install.

It is my own product. I started it because meditation helps me, but I find it hard to stay with. A picture, and something to do with my hands, work better for me. I build it with coding agents, and in five weeks it made five different promises.

This is Part I: from the launch on 14 October to 18 November 2026. There are no usage numbers in it yet. As I write, [reliev.app](https://reliev.app) still runs the October version, and everything new is on a staging server. Part II comes with traffic and numbers.

## Discovery: who else helps people feel better

Under every version there was one question: is this a meditation app? The answer depends on who you compare it with. I looked at what people already use when they feel bad, and I gave ChatGPT the prototype for an outside view: how to categorize it, whether people would pay, how to distribute it.

It came back with four categories: meditation and mindfulness, stress relief and breathwork, interactive digital wellness, and digital therapeutics. Reliev fits the first two. It would be most different in the third. It is not the fourth, which needs clinical evidence and medical-device rules. One line from that review stayed with me: "Calm teaches meditation. Breathwrk guides breathing. TRIPP creates immersive environments. Reliev could make emotional regulation a short, tangible, interactive action."

The closest products are not the biggest ones: Breathwrk for short breathing exercises, Lumenate for light you watch with your eyes closed, Finch for a pet that brings you back. And for someone anxious right now, the real alternative is not an app at all: a breathing video, a walk, the feed.

The jobs said the same. An agent's audit listed seven. The strongest was not "calm me down" but "a way in that isn't meditation": I know breathing helps, but I can't sit with it. That was my own story, and the product only said it in a FAQ.

At launch, Reliev had its own category: transitions. It set the product apart, but nobody searches for a transition. On 15 November I wanted to grow through TikTok and Reels, and instead of choosing a promise, the agents built several front doors on the same app. On 16 November an agent's audit said that was the problem: four promises, and slow to help. It recommended "in-the-moment anxiety relief", a small shelf next to Rootd and DARE. I chose "a meditation app with superpowers" instead, and accepted the comparison with Calm and Headspace.

Two days later I asked an agent for ten prototypes of interactive emotional regulation, built without looking at what we had: a pendulum, a worry jar, a map where you place your feeling, a scribble you calm down. Then the real question: a few consistent metaphors, or a broad catalog where almost anything fits? **I chose broad, and let usage decide.**

Going broad has a price, and I want it written down. A catalog is harder to say in one line: "personal emotional regulator", like "transitions", is a phrase nobody searches for. It gives more choice to someone who is struggling, which is what the cut of 16 November tried to avoid. And with little traffic, 21 sessions split the sample 21 ways, so it takes longer to tell a good session from a lucky one. The catalog is a research tool as much as a product, and it may get narrow again.

## Experience: playful on purpose

The launch looked like a screensaver: dark, with a purple glass sphere in a nebula. Beautiful, and serious. The new front door is flat colour and round shapes on an iris blue ground, with yellow buttons and a mascot.

The playful look is a decision, for three reasons. The first is sharing: if distribution has to live inside the product, people must be happy to show it. A brand that is warm and a little funny is easier to send to a friend than a dark, clinical one. Headspace showed that with its drawn characters, and it was my reference from the meditate door on. The second is a mascot: a playful system can carry a character. The third is range. A broad catalog needs a system loose enough for a breathing ring, a shooting star and a fire, and strict enough that all of them look like one product.

<Shot
  title="Lev, the mascot: it feels what you feel, then shows you the way back"

Lev is the sun dot from the logo, come alive. It shows how you feel, at the check-in and on the front door, and never decorates a session.

The rules are written down for people and for agents. Ten colours and no others, each with a meaning: coral is the hard end, sun is energy that works for you, mint is settled. One gesture per phase: first show where you are, then change it. Nothing can be failed, and the change shows early. The words have rules too: no engine words like "particles" or "nucleus", checked by a script. And the English is written for people who learned it as a second language, because many of the people it is for did.

You find it in two places: in a section of the front door, and at the end of a session that helped. You pick how the other person feels, sign it with your name if you like, and send it through the phone's share menu, WhatsApp, Telegram, Messages or email, or post it on X, Facebook, Threads or LinkedIn. ## Measurement: one arc, the same events

## Beyond the product

When a version costs an hour, changing direction twice in a week is affordable. That changes how I decide. A decision that is cheap to undo can be made with less evidence, so I make it, ship it to staging and look. The risk is to confuse moving with learning. Five promises in five weeks, with no real users, are five opinions, most of them mine.

## What I take from it

- **A category is a bet on who you will be compared with.** "Meditation" meant a comparison with Calm and Headspace. "Personal emotional regulator" brings nobody yet, which is good for being different and bad for being found.
- **Put distribution where the product just worked.** Papermark grows through the links its customers send. If RelievApp grows, it will be through the sessions people give, right after one helped them.
- **Breadth needs stricter rules, not looser ones.** The design system is what makes 21 sessions one product.
- **Next, in Part II:** production gets the new version, traffic arrives, and the numbers say which sessions stay.
