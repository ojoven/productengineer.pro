## Who and what

[Miquel Palet](https://linkedin.com/in/miquelpalet), Miki on [Zernio's team page](https://zernio.com/about), left a computer engineering degree in Barcelona to help build Ucademy, then left Ucademy in 2024 to start again on his own. In August 2025 he launched a new product over a weekend. Thirteen months later it makes about €5 million a year in recurring revenue. He owns all of it and has not raised a euro.

**[Zernio](https://zernio.com) is a unified API for social media, ads and messaging, for developers who want to offer those features inside their own product.** With one integration, a product can publish, schedule, answer comments and DMs and read analytics on Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, Facebook and ten more networks, run campaigns on nine ad networks, and send WhatsApp, SMS and email. It comes as a REST API with eight SDKs, an [MCP server](https://docs.zernio.com/mcp) and a CLI. It used to be called Late.

What it sells is waiting time. Before your users can post to Instagram from your app, Meta has to approve your app. Zernio has done the approvals, and your users connect through its app instead.

I did not talk to Miquel. This piece comes from his conversation with Bernat Farrero on the [Itnig podcast](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vE2z1XrCA6o), out on 12 November. Like [Mamitsu](/in-practice/mamitsu/), the last product I profiled, Zernio is software for other software and for agents, but this one already has the numbers. I grouped it by territory and added what I would ask and test.

## Discovery: a weekend bet on developers, not marketers

After Ucademy, Miquel spent a year he calls lost, with no cash, trying things. One, a tool that fetches supplier invoices, reached €7,000 to €8,000 MRR and later sold for about €200,000, but it was a crowded idea. On Twitter he saw lots of people building social media schedulers as side projects. His twist was to sell the plumbing to developers instead of the tool to marketers. No market study, a weekend of building and, compared with everything else he had launched, "people wanted it" from day one.

The need is in the platforms' rules. Bernat, who only uses X, pushed back: on X there is no approval to wait for. True, Miquel said, while the account is your own. The trouble starts when your users connect theirs to your product.

Why not build it in-house? Time, and it is not their core. Zapier and n8n connect to the same platforms, but you still bring your own developer app. Metricool is the finished tool for a marketer: "we let other people build Metricool". The direct rival was Ayrshare, which owned the search results and, as he tells it, moved slowly. And Twilio has sold the same model for WhatsApp for years.

Who pays, in his order: SaaS products that embed Zernio white-labelled, often AI apps that generate images and want to publish them; agencies with their own platform; and last, people managing their own accounts, often from Claude: most of the accounts, the least revenue.

## Strategy: from lifetime deals to a price per account

The first revenue came from lifetime licences on AppSumo. "When you have no users, you prefer users who pay you once to no users who never pay you." About 2,000 licences, a bit over $200,000 in sales, about $100,000 for him, paid 90 days later. It funded the first ads and the first hire.

Tiers meant add-ons and upgrade friction, but the main reason was another.

<Quote

Now the price is per connected account: two free, then $6 a month each up to ten, $3 up to a hundred and $1 beyond, with messages, numbers and managed ads metered on top. **Net MRR expansion, what existing customers add each month, is now Zernio's main metric.** New products bill themselves, without a new checkout. Gross margin is around 87%, "without having optimised anything".

Bernat saw pressure from two sides: platforms that could cut Zernio out, and big customers that could build it in-house. The second has not happened that Miquel knows of. For the first, he stays compliant and tries to get as far up each platform as he can.

<Quote
  text="Zernio captures the cost of Meta's own inefficiency in approving apps. It is money Meta is leaving on the table."
  who="Bernat Farrero"
  context="Host of the Itnig podcast"

Miquel's answer is Twilio again: Meta lets it charge on top of WhatsApp's fees. Whether Meta allows the same on Instagram, "Meta will decide". **The business sits in a grey zone the platforms tolerate today.** And the moat is not the code: mapping endpoints is easy, he said himself.

What he will not build is a Metricool of his own: it would compete with his customers, with different churn and different sales. Instead, this year Zernio added ads, WhatsApp, phone numbers and commerce APIs, often after integrators asked. Ads are about 15% of accounts and get the most requests; few APIs cover them.

"Most features we no longer build for the UI, only for the API." The dashboard shows maybe half of it, mostly logs, keys and usage for debugging. The site states it as a rule: if it is not in the API, it does not ship.

Analytics too: Zernio stores and refreshes them, so one call returns a week across all of a user's accounts.

The volume is real: about 100,000 posts a day and a constant stream of platform webhooks. Those events used to run on cron jobs, "not a good way to handle many events", and were the first thing moved to Cloudflare queues.

## AI: an MCP server that comes with the API

"The MCP is a wrapper of the API that we generate automatically when the API is generated." So is the CLI. Agents get every endpoint at no extra cost: the site lists 496 MCP tools and 371 CLI commands. The API "has always been king", and they do not track which interface people use. End users mostly come through MCP, checking their campaigns from Claude or Codex, and some customers sell marketing on autopilot built on Zernio.

Inside the team, agents do much of the work. Miquel had not coded at Ucademy, and Cursor arrived just as he needed to start again. He answered the launch's support tickets with Claude, and Claude writes the migrations, with someone making sure nobody notices. Asked what money would do for the product, he said more people was not the answer.

## Operations: 300 tickets a day and the cloud bill

The AppSumo launch brought 300 support tickets a day. Who answered? "Me." Three months in he hired Pau, a developer, and they took turns. The third hire, Elean, is a developer who only does support.

Zernio started on Vercel and is moving to Cloudflare because "Vercel at our scale is very expensive". The queues went first; the whole API is next, with zero downtime and without a single user noticing. Free accounts stay cheap, with one exception: X's API is paid, so connecting X asks for a card and Zernio passes the cost through.

Zernio also publishes [its production numbers](https://zernio.com/open), failures included: "APIs break. Tokens expire. Platforms return 500s at 2am."

The episode did not cover how nine people keep up with the changes of some 25 platforms. The site says those changes "are ours to absorb". How, is the first thing I would ask him.

## Distribution: AppSumo, Google and a name you can search for

Posting the launch on LinkedIn and the like did little: "nobody cares, your ideal customer is not there". AppSumo brings the demand itself, writing to its own list. He launched there on 5 August 2025.

Then search. **About 40% of new business comes from organic Google, on long-tail searches like "social media API" or "Meta app review".** Ayrshare owned those; Zernio now ranks above it on many. Landing pages and link building helped early; what worked best was users posting about Zernio and linking to it. A writer outside the team writes only blog posts, meant to be useful. They pay for spots in "best Meta API" rankings and built Zapier and Make integrations partly for the link. He had learned the limits on the invoice tool: 50,000 AI-written posts, then Google dropped the site from its index.

The [rebrand from Late](https://zernio.com/rebrand) was for search too: "Try searching 'Late API' and you'll understand: generic words make terrible brand names."

Search has a ceiling in a niche, so there is paid: €50,000 to €60,000 a month, 70% on Google, bringing about a quarter of the new MRR. Competitor keywords convert cheapest, which explains the [comparison pages](https://zernio.com/compare) and, for Ayrshare's users, a drop-in SDK with the same method signatures.

## Retention: the bigger the customer, the less it leaves

Has an integrator ever outgrown Zernio and replaced it? "Not that I know of. The bigger the platform, the more churn drops." The review is not a one-off. Adding ads means another review with Meta; posting more videos means another quota increase with YouTube. The need comes back with every feature.

What stayed with me:

### The beliefs he left Ucademy to test

At 18, during COVID, he sold an entrance exam course with his sister and a friend: €30,000 in a few weeks, nearly all of it spent on ads, teachers and taxes. Ucademy's founders, Pablo and Ramiro, bought the content, and he joined them in Madrid, left his degree and became a late co-founder. He says he learned most from them: coming from a technical background, you often only learn technical things. He left in February 2024, at 60 to 70 people, to test whether he could build a company without venture capital, less dependent on paid ads, remote. "I want hypergrowth, though not necessarily with VCs."

### A million euros he would not know where to put

Offered a hypothetical million, he would put it in paid: not the best channel, but the most predictable. SEO would not know what to do with it, and product would be hurt by it: "I don't feel the bottleneck is that we need more people".

### Not for sale

If he sold, he would build something similar the next day with the same people. So there are no stock options and no dividends, everything goes back into growth, and in five years he sees himself in "the same position".

## What I take from it

- **When code is cheap, sell what is slow.** Miquel says it himself: endpoints are easy. Zernio sells the months of reviews, quotas and token refreshes every developer would otherwise go through, done once and shared. In [Mamitsu's profile](/in-practice/mamitsu/), Unai said code is solved if you pay; Zernio is a business built on the parts that are not code. I want to look at my own products for the slow part someone would pay to skip.
- **Charge on the unit that grows with the customer.** Per-account pricing made expansion the main metric and lets each new product bill itself. If I start charging for one of my products, the first question is which unit grows when the customer does well.
- **Tag the segment at signup, from day one.** Doing everything and watching what worked got Zernio to €5M ARR, and I would not argue with that. But the 2% that brings half the revenue showed up a weekend before a podcast. One question at signup, or a rule on the first API calls, is cheap and sharpens every later number. It is the same suggestion I made to Unai, about where users come from.
