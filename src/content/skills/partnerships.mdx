## Why it matters for a product engineer

Partners reach customers you cannot reach alone: through their products, their audiences and their
sales teams. Integrations are the most durable kind, because once a customer connects two products,
the connection works for them every day. For a platform, partners also build what its own team never
will. Each integration is also an API to maintain, so product engineers decide which partnerships the
product can support.

## How to do it

1. List the tools your customers already use, and find where data must flow between them and your
   product.
2. Build the integrations with the most customer demand first, or open an API so partners can build
   them.
3. Treat the public API as a product: versions, docs, test accounts and notice before changes.
4. Compare retention and expansion for customers who use integrations with those who do not.
5. Give partners a reason to invest: distribution, revenue share or joint marketing.

## Common mistakes

- Announcing partnerships that no customer uses.
- Breaking changes to an API that partners depend on.
- Integrations built for a launch, then left without an owner.
- Counting installs, not active connections.

## Example

When Shopify opened its App Store about 15 years ago, it was a small list of tools. Today it lists
more than 21,000 apps, and active app installs grew by nearly 20% in the past year. In 2025, Shopify
paid more than $1.3 billion to the developers who build in its ecosystem. Shopify estimates that its
partner ecosystem generates $6.86 for every dollar Shopify earns.
