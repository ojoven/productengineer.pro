## Why it matters for a product engineer

An active user can mean anyone who opened the app, or someone who got what they came for. The first
definition makes numbers look good and hides problems. The second shows whether the product delivers
value. Product engineers write the events and queries behind these numbers, so they decide which
definition the team really sees.

## How to do it

1. Name the action that delivers the product's value, such as a finished lesson, a sent invoice or a
   completed order.
2. Count a user as active only when they did that action in the period.
3. Choose the period from how often the need comes back: a day, a week or a month.
4. Write the definition down, and use the same event in dashboards, experiments and reports.
5. Keep the login count as a second metric. A large gap between the two shows people who come and
   leave without value.

## Common mistakes

- Counting logins, page views or app opens as use.
- Counting actions that happen automatically, such as background syncs.
- A definition that changes between teams or between reports.
- Choosing the action that is easiest to track, not the one that means value.

## Example

Uber's main user metric does not count people who only open the app. It counts Monthly Active
Platform Consumers: unique people who completed a ride or received a delivery at least once in a
month. A person who uses both rides and delivery counts once. In the third quarter of 2026, Uber
reported 215 million of them and 4.06 billion trips. That is about 6.3 trips per person each month.
