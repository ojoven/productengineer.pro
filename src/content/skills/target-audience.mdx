## Why it matters for a product engineer

A target audience makes every decision smaller. Which platform first, which integration, which
default, which words on the first screen: each one is easier when you know who it is for. A product
for everyone has to be good at everything, and a small team cannot build that.

## How to do it

1. Start from what the product does best, and find the people who care most about that.
2. Describe them by situation and need, not only demographics. See
   [user segments](/skills-map/discovery/user-understanding/user-segments/).
3. Check that you can reach them: where they read, search and buy.
4. Choose one group first. You can add others later, from a position of strength.
5. Write it in one sentence and use it to say no to features, channels and copy.
6. Check it against real users every few months: who stays, who pays, who recommends you.

## Common mistakes

- "Everyone who…", which is not a choice.
- Choosing the biggest group instead of the one that needs you most.
- An audience you cannot reach with your channels.
- A target audience on the strategy slide and a different one on the home page.

## Example

Mamitsu is for developers of apps and agents, and its home page says so in one line. Inside that
group, Unai Esteibar chose a first niche: marketing teams that check whether sponsored videos really
mention their brand. RelievApp chose people who want what meditation gives but cannot sit still with
it, most of all when they are anxious.
