## Why it matters for a product engineer

AI output is sometimes wrong, and it looks just as confident when it is. The interface has to make
that safe: people must see what the system will do, judge it, and stay in control. For a product
engineer the design is in the interaction, not in the model: what is a suggestion and what is done,
and how easily a person can say no.

## How to do it

1. Tell people what the feature can do and where it fails, before they rely on it.
2. Make the status of AI output visible. A suggestion should look different from accepted work.
3. Make accepting a deliberate act, and rejecting cost nothing.
4. Let people take part of an answer, ask for another one, or edit it.
5. For actions with effects, such as sending, paying or deleting, show what will happen and ask before
   doing it. Make it possible to stop and undo.
6. Show where an answer came from when people need to check it.

## Common mistakes

- Presenting a guess in the same style as a fact.
- AI actions with effects that happen without a confirmation.
- Only one answer, with no way to ask again or edit.
- Hiding the AI, so people do not know what to check.
- Copying a pattern from a lab's product that changes every few weeks. Mamitsu's maker notes these
  patterns are not settled yet.

## Example

GitHub Copilot's code suggestions are a clear pattern for uncertain output. A suggestion appears as
grey text that is not yet code. Pressing Tab accepts it. Typing on ignores it. People can see other
suggestions, accept part of one, and always edit the result. The suggestion never becomes code without
a deliberate key press.
