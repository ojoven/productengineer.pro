## Why it matters for a product engineer

Availability is the share of time, or of requests, in which the product works for users. Each extra
nine costs much more than the last: more redundancy, slower releases, more people on call. Past some
point, users cannot notice the difference, because their own phone or network fails more often than
your service does. Product engineers choose the target with the business, and they design the system to
meet it.

## How to do it

1. Measure availability from the user's side, such as the share of successful requests, not only
   whether servers are up.
2. Choose the target from what users need and what an outage costs, not from what sounds impressive.
3. Turn the target into an error budget: the downtime you can accept in a period.
4. Spend the budget on releases and experiments, and slow down when it runs out.
5. Set different targets for different parts of the product, such as checkout and reports.

## Common mistakes

- Aiming for 100%, which stops all change.
- Counting a server as up while users get errors.
- One target for the whole product, whatever each part does.
- Promising customers a higher target than you meet internally.

## Example

Google's SRE book explains how the target depends on the users. For an enterprise service such as
Google Apps for Work, it suggests an external quarterly target of 99.9%, with a stronger internal
target. When Google bought YouTube in 2006, it set a lower target for YouTube than for its enterprise
products, because YouTube was changing fast and new features mattered more. The book also notes that a
user on a 99% reliable smartphone cannot tell 99.99% from 99.999%.
