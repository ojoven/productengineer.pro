## Why it matters for a product engineer

App stores, marketplaces, integration directories and launch sites put a product in front of people
who are already looking. Each has its own rules for listings and ranking, and many of those rules are
technical: limits on text, screenshot sizes, integration review. Today the same listings are also read
by AI answers, so what a directory says about your product can decide whether you are recommended.

## How to do it

1. List the places your users browse: the App Store or Google Play, integration directories such as
   Zapier, marketplaces, and the comparison pages that rank for your category.
2. Read each platform's rules, and write the listing within them, as you would for a search result.
3. Show the product in use: screenshots and short videos of the main task, not the logo.
4. Keep every listing consistent with your own site: the same name, price and description.
5. Test listing elements where the platform allows it, such as icons and screenshots.
6. Track installs or sign-ups per platform, and keep the listings that bring users who stay.

## Common mistakes

- A listing written once at launch and never updated.
- Screenshots of the interface with no hint of what it does.
- Different facts on different platforms, which confuses people and AI answers.
- Paying for a place in a list nobody reads.

## Example

Zernio built Zapier and Make integrations partly for the link, and pays for places in "best API"
rankings. Mamitsu's maker watches the directories that AI answers cite. When a directory disagrees
with a product's own site, the model cannot tell which version to believe.
([Zernio](/in-practice/zernio/), [Mamitsu](/in-practice/mamitsu/))
