## Why it matters for a product engineer

Scalability is knowing where your system will break as usage grows, and how you will move that limit
when you need to. Building for a million users before you have a hundred wastes months. Not knowing
your limits at all means finding them during your best week. The skill is scaling just in time.

## How to do it

1. Write down the expected load for the next year: users, requests, data, peaks.
2. Find the first bottleneck: often the database, a third-party rate limit, or a job that runs
   inside a request.
3. Measure it with a load test that looks like real traffic, before customers find it.
4. Fix the bottleneck with the simplest change: an index, a cache, a queue, a bigger machine.
5. Keep the parts that grow separate, so you can scale one without the rest.
6. Test again after each big change, and on a schedule.

## Common mistakes

- Microservices and sharding for a product with a few hundred users.
- Load tests that call one endpoint in a loop, nothing like real use.
- Forgetting the limits of the services you depend on.
- Scaling the web servers while the database is the limit.
- No idea of the current limit, only a feeling that it is "fine".

## Example

Zernio handles about 100,000 posts a day and a constant flow of webhooks from the platforms, with
six developers. The first scaling change was not a rewrite: platform events moved from cron jobs to
queues. Mamitsu's maker runs load tests every day, because holding load was his condition for selling
the product. ([Zernio](/in-practice/zernio/), [Mamitsu](/in-practice/mamitsu/))
