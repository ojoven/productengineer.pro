## Why it matters for a product engineer

Some rules apply to a product whatever its team thinks of them: data protection, health and finance
rules, accessibility, and now AI. Many of them turn into engineering work, such as access logs,
deletion, encryption and breach detection. Under the GDPR, for example, a company must report most
personal data breaches within 72 hours. Engineers do not need to be lawyers, but they need to know
when a feature touches a rule, and when to ask one.

## How to do it

1. List the rules that apply: where your users are, what data you hold, and which industry you serve.
2. Translate each rule into concrete requirements, with someone who knows the law.
3. Build the requirements into the system: logs, permissions, retention, consent, accessible
   interfaces.
4. Keep evidence as you go. Audits ask for proof, not intentions.
5. Watch new rules early, such as the EU AI Act and the European Accessibility Act.
6. Ask for legal advice before you ship anything you are unsure about.

## Common mistakes

- Treating compliance as paperwork after the product is built.
- Copying another company's privacy policy.
- Claiming a certificate you do not have.
- Assuming a rule does not apply because the company is small.

## Example

Papermark sells to deal teams, so its compliance is part of the product. It has a SOC 2 Type II
report from an independent auditor and ISO 27001 certification, and supports GDPR, HIPAA and CCPA
requirements. Its home page shows the audits above the headline.
