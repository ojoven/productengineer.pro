## Why it matters for a product engineer

Many activation problems are technical: slow pages, flows that break in one browser, emails that
never arrive, error messages nobody understands. A design review does not show them, but error logs
and real-user data do. Product engineers can see both the friction and the cause, so they are the
right people to find and fix it.

## How to do it

1. Measure performance on real users' devices, not only in the lab, and send it to your analytics
   with the funnel events.
2. Split activation by browser, device, connection and country, and look for the outliers.
3. Track client errors, failed requests and undelivered emails in the first session.
4. Fix the biggest causes first. For slow pages, the usual ones are render-blocking JavaScript,
   large images and work done in the browser that the server could do.
5. Prove the effect with an A/B test where the only difference is speed or reliability.

## Common mistakes

- Testing only on fast laptops and office Wi-Fi.
- Error messages that say "Something went wrong" and nothing more.
- Treating performance as a technical score, not as a business metric.
- Shipping a fix without checking that the field data improved.

## Example

Vodafone ran an A/B test on a landing page that received paid traffic. The two versions looked and
worked the same. Version A moved a widget's rendering from the browser to the server, rendered the
critical HTML on the server and optimized images. Its Largest Contentful Paint (LCP), measured on
real users' devices, was 31% better. Version A had 8% more sales.
