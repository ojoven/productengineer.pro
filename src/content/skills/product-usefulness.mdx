## Why it matters for a product engineer

People keep using a product because it solves their problem, again and again. Usefulness is the
foundation of retention. Reminders, streaks and rewards can bring people back to value, but they
cannot create it. Casey Winters lists three ways to raise retention, and the first is to make the
product more valuable. For a product engineer, that means work on the job itself: speed, reliability,
and fewer steps for the task people repeat.

## How to do it

1. Name the job people hire the product for, and how often that job naturally happens.
2. Measure how many days a month people use the product, not only how many use it.
3. Talk to people who stayed and people who left about the job, not about features.
4. Improve the repeated task: make it faster, more reliable, or able to save work for next time.
5. Remove a reminder in a test, and see who comes back anyway.

## Common mistakes

- Treating notifications as a retention strategy.
- Comparing retention with products that have a different natural frequency.
- Adding features for new users while the core task stays slow.
- Reading high usage among power users as proof for everyone.

## Example

Instagram sent daily digest notifications about stories, chosen by a model that predicted clicks. The
team saw that many went to very active users, who would see those stories anyway. So they built a
model of the notification's added effect, and stopped sending to people who would come back without
it. They sent substantially fewer notifications, and saw no fall in engagement.
