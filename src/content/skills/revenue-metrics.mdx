## Why it matters for a product engineer

Revenue metrics connect product work to the money that pays the team. Monthly recurring revenue
(MRR), average revenue per account and net revenue retention show whether customers pay more as they
get more value. An engineer who can read them understands which work matters most, and can build the
billing data that makes them correct.

## How to do it

1. Track MRR and split its change into parts: new, expansion, contraction and churn.
2. Calculate average revenue per account, and look at its spread. A few large accounts can carry the
   average.
3. Measure net revenue retention: this month's revenue from last year's customers, divided by what
   they paid then. Above 100% means existing customers grow faster than they leave.
4. Split every metric by plan, segment and channel.
5. Get the numbers from the billing system, not from a spreadsheet updated by hand.
6. Look at them every month next to product metrics, so you can connect a release to a change.

## Common mistakes

- Counting one-time payments or annual prepayments as monthly recurring revenue.
- Looking only at new revenue and ignoring churn.
- Averages that hide a few large customers.
- Revenue numbers that differ between dashboards.
- Celebrating growth that comes from price rises while usage falls.

## Example

Papermark publishes its annual recurring revenue (ARR), and each million came faster than the last:
18 months to the first, six to the second, four to the third. Zernio's main metric is net MRR
expansion, what existing customers add each month, because its price grows with each customer's
connected accounts. ([Papermark](/in-practice/papermark/), [Zernio](/in-practice/zernio/))
