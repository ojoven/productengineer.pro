## Why it matters for a product engineer

People do not sign up or pay for something they do not trust. Trust signals answer the question that
comes just before the commitment: is this safe, and is it real? Engineers build many of them:
security pages, clear prices, a working status page, and payment forms that look and behave like the
real thing.

## How to do it

1. Find the moment of doubt: the sign-up, the payment, the first upload of private data.
2. Write the question the user has at that moment, such as "Will my data be safe?" or "Is there a
   real company behind this?"
3. Answer it with evidence they can check: certifications with links, named customers, real reviews,
   a price with nothing hidden.
4. Put the answer where the doubt is, not only on a separate page.
5. Make it true and keep it true. A status page that says "operational" during an outage costs more
   trust than no status page.

## Common mistakes

- Invented testimonials, logos or numbers. In the EU they are also unlawful.
- Badges with no link to anything.
- Hidden costs that appear at the last step.
- Trust signals on the home page, and none at the moment of payment.

## Example

Papermark hosts confidential documents for deals. The first thing its home page says is not what it
does: it shows its security certifications, then the headline, then the number of customers and a
rating. For someone about to upload a company's financials, that is the order of their questions.
