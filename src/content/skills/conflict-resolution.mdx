## Why it matters for a product engineer

Teams that care about their work disagree: about priorities, designs and risks. That is useful,
because disagreement finds the weak points in a plan. It becomes a problem when it turns personal,
never ends, or is settled by whoever has the most power. A product engineer works with many roles, so
turning a disagreement into a decision is part of the job. The focus moves from positions to goals
and evidence, and then to a clear decision that everyone supports.

## How to do it

1. Restate the other person's position until they agree that you understood it.
2. Find the shared goal, and the exact point where you differ: facts, values or predictions.
3. Look for evidence that could settle it: data, a prototype, a small test.
4. Agree who decides if you still disagree, and how.
5. Once it is decided, commit, and agree when you will review the decision.

## Common mistakes

- Arguing about solutions before agreeing on the problem.
- Letting the loudest or most senior person win by default.
- Avoiding the conflict until it becomes personal.
- Using "disagree and commit" to end a discussion that never happened.

## Example

From the start, Python was led by its creator, Guido van Rossum, as "Benevolent Dictator for Life".
In July 2018, after a hard fight over one proposal, PEP 572, he stepped down and did not name a
successor. The core developers had to design their own process. PEP 13, written in December 2018,
created a steering council of 5 people, elected by the core team after each feature release. Its
mandate: seek consensus before acting, and act as a "court of final appeal" only when other methods
have failed.
