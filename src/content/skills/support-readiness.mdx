## Why it matters for a product engineer

Support hears about problems first. A customer writes a ticket minutes after something breaks, often
before any alert. If support knows what changed and whom to call, that ticket becomes an early
warning. If not, it waits in a queue. A product engineer who briefs support before a release gets
faster signals and calmer customers.

## How to do it

1. Before a release, tell support what changes, for whom, and when.
2. Give them the help articles, the known issues and the answers to the questions you expect.
3. Agree on an escalation path: which tickets go to the team, how, and who is on call.
4. Tag tickets about the release, so you can count them and see patterns.
5. Make sure customers can still reach support when the product itself is down, and keep customer
   contacts somewhere the product cannot delete.
6. Read the first tickets after the release yourself.

## Common mistakes

- Support learns about a change from the customers.
- A help center that describes the old interface.
- No way to link a ticket to the release that caused it.
- A support form that only works for signed-in users of a working product.

## Example

On 5 April 2022, a maintenance script at Atlassian deleted 883 sites, the products of 775
customers. The first support ticket came 8 minutes later. Some customer contact details had been
deleted too, so affected customers could not file tickets as usual, and Atlassian could not easily reach
them. For some customers, the outage lasted up to 14 days.
