## Why it matters for a product engineer

Expansion revenue comes from customers who get more value: more seats, a higher plan, add-ons. When the
product drives it, the upgrade follows real use, at the moment the customer needs more. Net revenue
retention above 100% means revenue from existing customers grows even with no new customers. Product
engineers build the usage limits, the billing rules and the upgrade prompts, so they shape whether
expansion feels fair.

## How to do it

1. Choose an expansion unit that grows with value, such as active seats, projects or usage.
2. Find who is near a limit, and show the upgrade at that moment, with what they get.
3. Make billing follow real use, so customers are not afraid to invite more people.
4. Let admins see usage and cost before the bill arrives.
5. Track net revenue retention by cohort, and the share of revenue that comes from expansion.

## Common mistakes

- Limits that block work in the middle of a task.
- Charging for seats nobody uses, so admins stop inviting people.
- Upgrade prompts shown to users who cannot buy.
- Surprise bills after automatic upgrades.

## Example

Slack's Fair Billing Policy, for plans bought on its website, charges paid workspaces only for active
members. A member who accepts an invitation is billed from that day. If they do not use Slack for more
than 28 days, Slack marks them inactive and adds a prorated credit for the unused time. When they start
again, billing restarts for the rest of the period. So a company can invite whole teams without paying
for people who never use the product.
