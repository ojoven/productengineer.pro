## Why it matters for a product engineer

Product work fails in four ways: nobody wants it (value), people cannot use it (usability), it cannot
be built well (feasibility), or it does not work for the business (viability). Engineers usually see
feasibility risk first. Naming all four tells you what to test before you build, and what to watch
after you ship.

## How to do it

1. For each important piece of work, write what must be true for it to succeed.
2. Sort those assumptions into the four risks: value, usability, feasibility, business.
3. Mark how much you know about each one: measured, observed, reported, or only assumed.
4. Test the riskiest assumption first, with the cheapest test that could prove it wrong.
5. For risks you cannot test, such as a platform's decision, plan what you would do if it happened.
6. Review the list when something changes: a new competitor, a new rule, a big customer.

## Common mistakes

- Treating uncertainty as a reason to avoid the work, instead of a reason to test first.
- Only looking at technical risk.
- A risk list that never changes the plan.
- Forgetting risks that come from outside: platforms, regulators, large customers.

## Example

Zernio's biggest risks sit outside the product. The platforms could close the door, and the largest
customers could build the integrations themselves. Miquel Palet's answers mix what he knows with what
he cannot know: no big customer has built it in-house, as far as he knows, and whether Meta lets
Zernio charge on Instagram, as it lets Twilio charge on WhatsApp, "Meta will decide".
