## Why it matters for a product engineer

Content brings people who have the problem your product solves, at the moment they search for an
answer. Unlike ads, a useful page keeps working after you stop paying. Engineers are well placed to
write it: technical guides, honest comparisons, documentation and small free tools are content that
ranks, gets shared and gets cited by AI answers.

## How to do it

1. Start from the questions your users ask before they find you, in their words. Search data,
   support tickets and sales calls show them.
2. Write one page per question, and answer it on the page, not after a sign-up.
3. Prefer formats where engineers have an advantage: guides with working code, comparison pages, docs
   and free tools.
4. Use AI to draft and to find gaps, and have a person who knows the subject read every page before
   it goes out.
5. Link each page to the next step in the product.
6. Measure what each page brings: sign-ups and activated users, not only visits.

## Common mistakes

- Publishing at volume with AI and no review. Search engines treat it as spam.
- Writing about the company instead of the reader's problem.
- Content with no link to the product, which brings visitors who never sign up.
- Judging content after a month. It takes longer to rank.

## Example

Before Zernio, its founder ran a tool that published 50,000 AI-written posts, and Google dropped the
site from its index. At Zernio, one writer outside the team writes only blog posts meant to be useful.
Mamitsu's maker uses an agent to draft guides and comparison pages from gaps in AI answers, and he
reads everything before it goes out. ([Zernio](/in-practice/zernio/), [Mamitsu](/in-practice/mamitsu/))
