## Why it matters for a product engineer

A funnel shows where people stop. Funnel optimization chooses the step that loses the most people,
finds out why, and fixes that step first. Some reasons are outside the flow, such as price, and some
are problems a team can fix. A product engineer also checks that a gain survives further down the
funnel.

## How to do it

1. Build the funnel from events, with a conversion window that fits the product.
2. Find the step that loses the most people in absolute numbers, not only in percent.
3. Find out why, with research: surveys, session replays and usability tests.
4. Separate the reasons you can fix, such as errors and forced accounts, from the ones you cannot,
   such as people who are only browsing.
5. Test the fix, and check the steps after it, such as activation or refunds.

## Common mistakes

- Improving every step a little instead of fixing the worst one.
- Treating people who were only browsing as a problem to solve.
- Hiding costs until the last step, to raise early conversion.
- Celebrating more signups that never activate.

## Example

Baymard Institute averages 50 studies to a documented cart abandonment rate of 70.22%. In its survey
of US online shoppers, 42% had abandoned a cart because they were just browsing. Among the other
reasons, extra costs such as delivery, tax and fees were the most common, at 40%. Slow delivery,
distrust of the site with card details, forced account creation, a long checkout and site errors
were each named by 17% to 20%.
