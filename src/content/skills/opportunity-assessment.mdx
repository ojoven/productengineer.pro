## Why it matters for a product engineer

Most ideas are not worth building, and building is the most expensive way to find out. A short
assessment asks five questions: what problem, for whom, how big, why now, and how will we know it
worked. Engineers who ask them get better work, and can say no with reasons.

## How to do it

1. Name the problem and who has it, in one or two sentences, without the solution.
2. Estimate how many people have it and how often, and how
   [severe](/skills-map/discovery/problem-identification/problem-severity/) it is.
3. Ask why now. What changed in the market, in the technology or in your product?
4. Ask why you. Does it fit what the business already does, and how it sells?
5. List what people use today instead, including doing nothing.
6. Decide how you will know it worked, before you start.

## Common mistakes

- Assessing only the opportunities you already like.
- Sizing from a market report, not from your own users.
- Ignoring fit, so you find a good opportunity for someone else.
- No "how will we know", so every result looks like success.

## Example

Zernio could build its own Metricool, a finished social media tool for marketers. It would compete with
its own customers, with different churn and different sales. Instead it added ads, WhatsApp and
commerce APIs, often after integrators asked. Ads are used by about 15% of accounts, get the most
requests, and few APIs cover them. ([Zernio in In practice](/in-practice/zernio/))
