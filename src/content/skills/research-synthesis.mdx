## Why it matters for a product engineer

Research helps only when the team can act on it. Synthesis turns many notes into a few findings, each
with its evidence. Engineers who take part know the reasons behind what they build, and can question a
weak finding before it becomes a feature.

## How to do it

1. Analyse soon after each round of research, with everyone who watched it. GOV.UK suggests one hour
   of analysis for every two hours of research.
2. Write one observation per note: exactly what someone did or said, not what you think it means.
3. Sort the notes into groups, and let anyone move anyone's note until clear groups appear.
4. Write each finding as a short statement, such as "people think they can click the progress bar",
   not as a topic like "navigation".
5. Check each finding against earlier decisions, and say which ones it changes.
6. End with actions: what to change, what to test, what to research next. An opportunity solution
   tree connects each finding to the outcome you want.

## Common mistakes

- Topic labels instead of findings.
- Mixing what people did with what the researcher thinks it means.
- Synthesis by one person, alone, weeks later.
- A report that ends with themes and no decision.

## Example

On 18 November RelievApp's maker gave spoken feedback on ten prototypes. An agent kept the raw text and
structured it into a verdict per prototype, four ideas that cut across them, and a check against the
decisions of 16 November. The check showed one direct conflict. The maker chose the broad catalog, with
one condition: a strong design system for every session.
