## Why it matters for a product engineer

A launch is the moment when the most people arrive at once, and the moment the team knows least
about real load. Operational readiness checks, before that moment, that the system can hold the
traffic, show what is wrong, and recover. A short review prevents most launch-day surprises.

## How to do it

1. Estimate the load: the expected traffic and a worst case. Then ask what happens at ten times the
   worst case.
2. Load test the critical paths, and know which part breaks first.
3. Check the dependencies: quotas, rate limits and the capacity of every outside service.
4. Make sure alerts and dashboards cover the new feature, and that someone is on call.
5. Write the runbook: how to scale, how to degrade gracefully, how to roll back.
6. Launch in stages when you can, by region or by share of users.

## Common mistakes

- Planning capacity for the expected case only.
- A load test of the app, but not of the database or an outside API.
- Alerts that go to nobody during the launch.
- No plan to turn features off to save the core.

## Example

Pokémon GO launched first in Australia and New Zealand. Within 15 minutes, traffic was well past
Niantic's expectations, and Google's reliability engineers joined before the US launch the next day.
Before the launch in Japan, the teams upgraded the container cluster to add more than a thousand
nodes and changed the load balancer. Japan launched without incident, with three times the sign-ups
of the US launch.
