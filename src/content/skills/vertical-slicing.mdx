## Why it matters for a product engineer

When work is split by layer, first the database, then the API, then the interface, nothing works until
the end, and the hard problems appear late. A vertical slice goes through every layer to deliver one
narrow behaviour that works. Each slice can be tested, shipped and judged on its own, so the team
learns early, while learning is cheap.

## How to do it

1. Start from what a user can do, not from the components.
2. Look for what there are many of: workflow steps, operations, business rules, kinds of data,
   interfaces.
3. For a workflow, build the simple case from start to end first, then add the steps in the middle.
4. Split a story that says "manage" into its operations: create, edit, cancel.
5. Keep each slice small enough to finish in a few days, and useful enough that someone would notice.
6. Use a time-boxed spike only as a last resort, when you cannot split the work without learning
   something first.

## Common mistakes

- Slices by layer, named after components.
- Splitting a workflow one step at a time from the beginning, so nothing works from end to end for
  weeks.
- Slices so thin that none of them is worth showing to a user.
- A spike that quietly becomes the feature.

## Example

A client of Humanizing Work had a short story: a content manager publishes a news story to the
corporate website. The real workflow needed editorial and legal approval, and a review on a staging
site. The story became five, starting with direct publishing from end to end, then one story for each
step of the approval.
