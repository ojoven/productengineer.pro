## Why it matters for a product engineer

Most late projects are late because of one thing nobody looked at early: an unknown in the code, a
dependency on another team, a design question left open. Finding that part before the work starts,
and doing it first, is often the difference between shipping on time and not. Engineers usually see
these risks first, so raising them early is part of being reliable.

## How to do it

1. Before work starts, walk through the solution step by step and ask where it could take much longer
   than planned.
2. Look for the usual sources: new technology, data migrations, other teams, external APIs, design
   questions nobody has answered.
3. For each one, decide: settle it now, cut it, or test it first with a short spike.
4. Write down what is out of bounds, so the team does not drift into it.
5. Do the riskiest part first, while there is still time to change the plan.
6. Say it early when a risk becomes real. Bad news is cheaper on day two than on the last day.

## Common mistakes

- Starting with the easy, visible parts and leaving the unknown for last.
- Passing an unsolved design problem to the team under a deadline.
- Optimistic plans with no margin.
- Hiding a risk because it feels like admitting a mistake.

## Example

Basecamp found a hole in its plan for to-do groups before the team started: nobody had decided how
completed items would show. Changing that touched design, navigation and performance, so the people
shaping the project chose an answer in advance. Completed items stayed as they were, with the group
name added. Colour-coded groups were cut. At Mamitsu, a referee agent scores the risk of every pull
request, and the score decides who approves it.
