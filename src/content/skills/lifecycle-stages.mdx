## Why it matters for a product engineer

An early product needs learning and speed. A growing one needs reliability and scale. A mature one
needs efficiency. The right practices differ by stage. Knowing the stage stops a team from using
big-company process on a product with ten users, or startup shortcuts on a product that thousands of
people depend on.

## How to do it

1. Name the stage honestly, from evidence: users, revenue and growth. The classic stages are
   introduction, growth, maturity and decline.
2. Match the work to the stage. Introduction: talk to users, ship fast, measure the few things that
   decide whether the product lives. Growth: reliability, scale, onboarding and support. Maturity:
   efficiency, costs and keeping customers. Decline: earn what is left, migrate or retire.
3. Let process grow with the stage: reviews, documentation, on-call.
4. Watch for the move between stages, such as the gap between early adopters and the mainstream
   market.
5. Look at the stage again every few months. Stages change faster than habits.

## Common mistakes

- Heavy process before the product has found its market.
- No process when thousands of customers depend on you.
- Assuming every product will reach maturity.
- Keeping the strategy of the last stage.
