## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every action needs a visible answer. Without one, people click again, leave, or assume something
broke. Feedback is also a large part of how fast a product feels: a clear loading state or an
optimistic update can make a slow request feel quick. Most of it is engineering work, in states,
timing and error handling.

## How to do it

1. List every action in a flow and the answer it gets: what changes on screen, and when.
2. Answer within about a tenth of a second, even if only to show that the action was received.
3. For longer work, show progress, or show the result before the server confirms it and undo it if the
   request fails.
4. Confirm results in place, near the action, instead of with a distant message.
5. Make errors say what happened and what to do next.
6. Test on a slow network and a slow phone, where missing feedback becomes obvious.

## Common mistakes

- Buttons that do nothing visible until the request ends.
- A spinner for everything, with no idea of how long.
- Success messages that disappear before anyone reads them.
- Feedback only by colour.
- Optimistic updates that fail without telling anyone.

## Example

In RelievApp, every touch is answered with shape, sound or a haptic, and nothing can be failed. Each
session has one gesture per phase: first show where you are, then change it. In "Turn it into fuel",
you scribble over a red core, then spin the scribbles into an orbit. The last screen shows the change
as two numbers: 8 before, 4 after. ([RelievApp in In practice](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
