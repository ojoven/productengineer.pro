## Why it matters for a product engineer

Most work does not stop because it is hard. It stops because it waits: for a decision, a review,
access or another team. A product engineer who notices waiting work and moves it keeps the whole
team fast. Blockers that come back again and again point to a process worth fixing.

## How to do it

1. Make waiting visible. Mark blocked items on the board, with the reason and the date.
2. Name the person who can unblock each item, not the team.
3. Ask early and specifically: what you need, by when, and why it matters.
4. Offer to do the work that makes it easy to say yes: a short summary, a draft decision, a smaller
   change to review.
5. Escalate politely when a blocker passes its deadline.
6. Count blockers by type every few weeks, and fix the most common cause.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting quietly and starting something else.
- Asking a whole channel instead of one person.
- Large pull requests that nobody wants to start reviewing.
- Fixing the same blocker by hand every time.

## Example

Microsoft built Nudge for overdue pull requests. It predicts when each pull request should be
complete, ignores the ones with enough activity, and works out who the blocker is, the author or a
reviewer. Then it reminds only that person. In a randomized trial, it was tested on 147 repositories
at Microsoft.
