## Why it matters for a product engineer

The value proposition is the sentence that connects the problem to the product. It decides the first
screen, the onboarding and which features come first. If the team cannot say it in one or two
sentences, users will not understand it either, and engineers will build features that pull in
different directions.

## How to do it

1. Name the user and the situation, using your
   [target audience](/skills-map/strategy/product-positioning/target-audience/).
2. Write what they get, in their words, compared with what they use today.
3. Add the proof: a number, a demo, a before and after.
4. Test it where people decide: a [landing page](/skills-map/discovery/opportunity-validation/landing-page-tests/),
   an ad, the first message of a sales call.
5. Check that the product keeps the promise in the first minutes. A promise the product does not keep
   loses people faster than a weak one.
6. Change it when the product or the audience changes, and update every page that repeats it.

## Common mistakes

- Describing features instead of the result for the user.
- Words like "all-in-one" or "seamless" that every product uses.
- A promise for everyone, which speaks to nobody.
- Several promises at once, on different pages.
- A promise the first minutes of the product do not keep.

## Example

RelievApp launched with "Transition from anxious to calm", in a category of its own that nobody
searched for. In November it tried several promises on different front doors, and an audit found that
was the problem. On 18 November the promise became a place to go: "Whatever you feel, feel better",
with one button that finds a session for how you feel.
