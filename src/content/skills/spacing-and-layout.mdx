## Why it matters for a product engineer

Space shows structure. Things close together read as a group; things far apart read as separate.
A consistent spacing scale and a simple grid remove most visual mess. With flexbox and grid,
engineers can be very precise here, often more precise than a static mockup.

## How to do it

1. Choose a spacing scale with few steps, such as 4, 8, 16, 24, 32 and 48 pixels, and use only those
   values.
2. Put less space inside a group than between groups, so the eye sees the groups.
3. Align things to a few shared edges. Every extra edge adds noise.
4. Give each kind of content a reserved place on the screen, so it never covers something more
   important.
5. Let the layout follow the content: use grid for two dimensions and flexbox for one, with `gap`
   instead of margins on each child.
6. Check the result at phone width and at a wide width, with long and short content.

## Common mistakes

- Spacing values picked by eye, a different one in every component.
- The same space between everything, so nothing groups.
- Text placed over the subject of the screen.
- Layouts that only work with the example content from the design.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp found text on top of the subject in several scenes. In the breathing
scene a headline and a chip sat on the sphere; in the pain scene, text covered the figure's head and
chest. The fix it proposed was layout, not copy: reserved bands for text, so no text can sit over the
subject. ([RelievApp in In practice](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
