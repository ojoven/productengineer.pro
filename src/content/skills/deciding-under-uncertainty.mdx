## Why it matters for a product engineer

Most product decisions must be made before the facts are in: which feature to build, which market to
try, whether to rewrite a system. Waiting for certainty is also a decision, and often the most
expensive one. Jeff Bezos wrote that most decisions should be made with about 70% of the information
you wish you had, because if you wait for 90%, you are probably being slow. The skill is to act
anyway, while limiting how much you can lose.

## How to do it

1. Write the decision, the options and what you expect each one to produce.
2. Estimate how likely you are to be right, as a rough percentage, not "probably".
3. Limit the downside before you start: a budget, a time box, or a way back.
4. Name the signal that would change your mind, and when you will look for it.
5. Review the decision on that date, and judge the process, not only the result.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting for data that will never arrive.
- Treating a bad result as proof of a bad decision, or a good result as proof of a good one.
- Risking more than you can afford to lose on a first attempt.
- No date to review the decision, so it is never revisited.

## Example

Zernio's Miquel Palet left Ucademy in February 2024, when it had 60 to 70 people, to test whether he
could build a company without investors. He then spent a year without cash, trying ideas. One reached
€7,000 to €8,000 a month and later sold for about €200,000. In August 2025 he launched Zernio over a
weekend. Papermark's founders had savings from good jobs for their first year, and Marc counts five or
six failures before Papermark. ([Zernio](/in-practice/zernio/), [Papermark](/in-practice/papermark/))
