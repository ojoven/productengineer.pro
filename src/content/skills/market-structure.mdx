## Why it matters for a product engineer

The structure of a market decides what is realistic: which business model works, which channel
reaches people, and who can take your margin. For a product engineer it also decides the rules you
build under, such as an app store's fees or a regulator's evidence requirements.

## How to do it

1. List the players: who uses the product, who pays, who sells, and who sits in between, such as app
   stores, marketplaces and resellers.
2. Follow the money. Who pays whom, and which share does each one keep?
3. Find who holds the power: who owns the relationship with the customer, where switching is
   expensive, where network effects protect a leader.
4. Check how concentrated it is. A few big platforms and a long tail of small tools need different
   strategies.
5. Note where the category changes the rules, such as a health claim that turns an app into a
   medical device.
6. Decide where you sit on the map, and write what that means for your model and your channel.

## Common mistakes

- Mapping only direct competitors and forgetting the intermediaries.
- Ignoring the fees and policies of the platform you depend on.
- Thinking a fragmented market is easy. It can mean nobody has found a way to reach the customers.
- Stepping into a regulated category by accident, through one sentence of marketing copy.

## Example

An outside review of RelievApp mapped four categories of products that help people feel better:
meditation and mindfulness, stress relief and breathwork, interactive digital wellness, and digital
therapeutics. RelievApp fits the first two and would be most different in the third. It is not the
fourth, which needs clinical evidence and medical-device rules. Placing the products on two axes
showed an empty corner, which is both a chance and a warning.
