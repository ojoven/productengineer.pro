## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every choice, unfamiliar word and thing to remember costs attention. When the cost is too high, people
make mistakes or leave. Engineers decide much of it: how many fields a form has, what is pre-filled,
when validation runs and what the system remembers for the user. Lower cognitive load is often the
real reason a redesign feels better.

## How to do it

1. For each screen, count what it asks people to decide, read and remember. Remove what the task
   does not need.
2. Let the system remember: pre-fill what you already know, keep entered data after an error and
   show earlier choices.
3. Prefer recognition to recall. Show options, recent items and examples instead of asking people to
   type from memory.
4. Use the words your users use, and ask one thing per question.
5. Group related fields, use standard controls, and show the expected format before people type.
6. Validate a field when people have finished it, not while they are still typing.

## Common mistakes

- Hints inside the field as placeholder text, which disappears as soon as people type.
- Internal or technical words in labels.
- Negative questions that people must reverse in their head.
- A new kind of control where a standard one exists.

## Example

NN/g found that Udemy asked people to check a box in order not to receive promotional emails, and
checking it cleared the boxes above it. People must reverse the sentence in their head, and the
checkbox behaves like a radio button. NN/g suggests asking first whether people want promotional
emails, then which kinds.
