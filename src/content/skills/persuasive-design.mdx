## Why it matters for a product engineer

Persuasion techniques, such as social proof, defaults, framing and reciprocity, change what people do.
That is why they need care. They can help people make a choice they already want, or push them into
one they do not. Engineers build the parts: counters, badges, pre-selected options and the variants of
a test. Knowing the techniques also helps you see when a design crosses the line.

## How to do it

1. Write down the choice that is good for the user. Persuasion should help people do what they would
   choose with full information.
2. Use only true facts: real counts, real reviews and real limits on stock or time.
3. Test the exact words and images against a control. Intuition about persuasion is often wrong.
4. Measure the completed action, not the click.
5. Ask the questions for deceptive patterns: could people spend or share more than they intended?
   Could they miss another choice?
6. Keep only what people would still accept if they saw how it works.

## Common mistakes

- Placeholder ratings, user counts or press logos left on a page that gets traffic.
- Countdowns and "only 2 left" messages that are not true.
- Copying a competitor's technique without checking whether it is honest.
- Judging a variant by clicks when the goal is a completed action.

## Example

In 2013, the GOV.UK page shown after people bought a vehicle tax disc asked them to join the NHS Organ
Donor Register. The Behavioural Insights Team tested eight versions with 1,085,322 visitors and counted
completed registrations. A question about reciprocity worked best: about 96,000 more registrations a
year than the plain request. A stock photo of people made a social-proof message worse than the plain
request.
