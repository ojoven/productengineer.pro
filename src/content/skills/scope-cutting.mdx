## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every feature you add has to be built, tested, documented and maintained. Cutting scope is how a small
team ships something good on time. Engineers see the real cost of each requirement first, so they are
often in the best position to propose what to leave out.

## How to do it

1. Write the goal of the work in one sentence, and the time it is worth.
2. List every requirement, and for each one ask: what happens if we drop it? If the honest answer is
   "not much", drop it.
3. Split the rest into what the goal needs now, what can wait, and what you will not build.
4. Look for the parts that could blow up the timeline, and simplify or remove them before work starts.
5. Write down what you cut and why, so the decision is not reopened every week.
6. When time runs short, cut scope again rather than moving the date or the quality.

## Common mistakes

- Cutting quality, such as tests or error handling, instead of features.
- Keeping a feature because one customer asked for it once.
- Cutting silently, so people still expect the feature.
- Cutting the part that makes the product different.
- Never adding anything back, even after users ask for it.

## Example

Mamitsu does one thing: a link to a post from four platforms and a question go in, JSON comes out.
Unai Esteibar left out features built on YouTube transcripts and a generic scraper for any URL. In
RelievApp, an audit on 16 November led to cutting the catalog from 18 sessions to 12. Two days later
it grew to 21 again, on purpose, as a research tool.
