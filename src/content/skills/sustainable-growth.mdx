## Why it matters for a product engineer

Growth is sustainable when each customer pays back what it cost to win them in a reasonable time, and
enough customers stay to keep the base growing. Payback period is the key number. Growth that needs
more spend every month stops when the money stops. Product engineers improve both sides: cheaper
acquisition through the product, and better retention.

## How to do it

1. Calculate payback per channel: acquisition cost divided by monthly gross margin per customer.
2. Set a limit, such as 12 months for a small business product, and treat channels above it as
   experiments.
3. Grow spend only as fast as you can see its payback.
4. Invest in channels that cost little per extra customer: content, search, referrals, sharing.
5. Watch retention next to growth. Customers who leave early never pay back.
6. Plan for rising costs. Each channel gets more expensive as you spend more in it.

## Common mistakes

- Scaling spend before you know the payback per channel.
- Counting revenue instead of gross margin in payback.
- Growth that depends on one channel you do not control.
- Ignoring churn because new customers hide it.

## Example

Zernio and Papermark grew without investors, so growth had to pay for itself. Zernio's paid ads take
about ten months to pay back. Before spending more, its founder hired a developer who only runs
Zernio's own ads, through its own API, because paid felt "a bit out of control".
