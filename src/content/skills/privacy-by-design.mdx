## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every piece of personal data a product keeps is a risk: it can leak, be misused, or be demanded by
someone else. Privacy by design means making the private choice the default: collect less, keep it for
less time, and limit who can see it. It is much cheaper to do at the start than to add later, because
data models, logs and analytics all depend on it. It also builds trust, and it reduces what any
breach can expose.

## How to do it

1. For each field you plan to collect, write down why you need it and for how long.
2. Collect the minimum, and prefer data that does not identify a person.
3. Set deletion by default: logs, backups and analytics expire.
4. Encrypt sensitive data, and limit who and what can read it.
5. Make the private option the default, and let users choose to share more.

## Common mistakes

- Collecting data "in case it is useful later".
- Logging full request bodies, with personal data in them, to debug one problem.
- Deleting data from the database but keeping it for years in backups and analytics.
- Asking legal to review privacy after the feature is built.

## Example

Signal publishes the legal requests it receives for user data. In 2021, a grand jury subpoena from
California asked for, among other things, users' names, addresses and correspondence. Signal could not
provide any of it, because its servers never had it: messages, contacts, groups and profiles are
end-to-end encrypted. Its answer was the same as in 2016: the time each account was created and the date
it last connected. The response to the subpoena had been decided years earlier, in the design.
