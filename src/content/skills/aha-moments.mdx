## Why it matters for a product engineer

The aha moment is the first time someone sees why the product is worth their time. Users who reach
it tend to stay, and users who do not reach it tend to leave. Once you know the moment, you know what
the first run should lead to, and which steps before it you can cut. That makes it one of the most
useful facts an engineer can have about onboarding.

## How to do it

1. Compare users who stayed with users who left. Look for actions in the first days that the first
   group did much more often.
2. Check the timing: the action must happen early enough to matter, and before retention, not after.
3. Treat the pattern as a correlation. Power users do many things, and not all of them cause staying.
4. Test it: make the action easier or more visible for some new users, and see whether their
   retention changes.
5. If it does, design the first run to lead there, and measure the share of new users who reach it.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the aha moment in a meeting, with no data.
- Treating a correlation as the cause, and pushing every user through an action that does not help.
- An aha moment so late that most users never get there.
- One moment for every kind of user, when different segments find value in different places.

## Example

Calm's daily reminder was hidden in the settings, and fewer than 1% of users set one. On a hunch, the
team compared users who set a reminder with those who did not: retention was almost three times
higher. That could have been power users finding the setting, so Calm tested it. After the first
meditation, some new users saw a screen that offered a reminder.
