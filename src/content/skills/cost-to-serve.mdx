## Why it matters for a product engineer

Cost to serve is what one customer, or one unit of use, really costs: servers, third-party APIs, AI
calls and support time. It shows which plans make money and which lose it. Engineers can measure it,
because they know which feature calls which service. And AI features have made it urgent: one heavy
user can cost more than they pay.

## How to do it

1. Choose the unit: a customer, an account, a request, a document.
2. Add up the costs per unit: compute, storage, paid APIs, AI tokens, payment fees and support time.
3. Compare cost and revenue per unit, by plan and by kind of customer.
4. Find the expensive exceptions: one feature, one provider, a few heavy users.
5. Price them on purpose: pass the cost through, limit it, or put it in a higher plan.
6. Recalculate when a provider changes its prices.

## Common mistakes

- Average cost per customer, which hides the few that cost the most.
- Forgetting support time and payment fees.
- Free plans that include an expensive third-party call.
- AI features priced as if each call were free.

## Example

Zernio's first two connected accounts are free and cost it little. X is the exception: X charges for
its API. So connecting an X account asks for a card, and Zernio passes the cost of X's API through at
X's own rates, with no markup. ([Zernio in In practice](/in-practice/zernio/))
