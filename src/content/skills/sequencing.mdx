## Why it matters for a product engineer

A rank says what matters most. An order says what to do first. Some work makes everything after it
cheaper or safer: a measurement, a migration, a test of the riskiest assumption. Engineers often see
these orders first, because they know which change unlocks the others.

## How to do it

1. For each piece of work, write what it depends on and what it unlocks.
2. Put first the work that every later result depends on, such as measurement and data quality.
3. Then the work that teaches the most for its cost: the riskiest assumption, the smallest test.
4. Order independent work by cost of delay divided by duration (CD3), so short, urgent work goes first.
   When one item must wait for another, count the whole chain as its duration.
5. Group the plan as now, next and later, and commit only to "now".
6. Change the order when you learn something. The order is a tool, not a promise.

## Common mistakes

- Ordering by rank alone, and ignoring what unlocks what.
- Building on a measurement you do not trust yet.
- Starting many items at once, so nothing finishes.
- Treating "later" as a commitment.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp ended with "What to do, in order". The first item, before any more
traffic, was an honest rating. A faster first session came next. Depth on anxiety and pain, and a test
of the free plan and the trial, came after that; a door for pain came later.
