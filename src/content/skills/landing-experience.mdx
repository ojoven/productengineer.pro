## Why it matters for a product engineer

The first page a new visitor sees has a few seconds to say what the product does, for whom, and what
to do next. It is where the promise of an ad or a link meets the product. A product engineer
controls what loads first, how fast it loads and what the main button does, so this page is as much
engineering as copy.

## How to do it

1. Write one sentence that says what the product does and for whom, and put it first.
2. Give one main action, with a label that says what happens next.
3. Make the first screen load fast on a phone, on a slow connection.
4. Match the message to where visitors come from: the ad, the search or the post they clicked.
5. Test the parts that carry the most weight, such as the headline, the image and the button.
6. Measure what happens after the click too, not only the click.

## Common mistakes

- A clever headline that does not say what the product is.
- Several buttons with the same weight.
- Heavy video or animation that delays the first screen.
- Choosing the version the team likes best without testing it.

## Example

In December 2007, Barack Obama's campaign tested its splash page, the page that asked visitors to
sign up. It tried four buttons and six media, three images and three videos, in 24 combinations,
with 310,382 visitors. The campaign staff favoured one of the videos. In the test, every video did
worse than every image. The winner, a family photo with a "Learn More" button, raised the sign-up
rate from 8.26% to 11.6%, an improvement of 40.6%.
