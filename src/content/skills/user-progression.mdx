## Why it matters for a product engineer

People stay with a product longer when they can see themselves getting better at something. Levels,
streaks and unlocked features make that growth visible. They work when they track real skill or real
contribution, and they fail when they reward activity for its own sake. A product engineer decides
what the system counts, so they decide whether progress means anything.

## How to do it

1. Define progress by the outcome the user cares about: skills learned, tasks done well, trust earned.
2. Base it on signals that are hard to fake, such as approval from other people or tasks completed
   correctly.
3. Make the first steps quick, and space the later ones further apart.
4. Let each level unlock something useful, such as a new ability or more responsibility, not only a
   badge.
5. Watch for gaming, and check that users at higher levels really are more skilled or more active.

## Common mistakes

- Points for actions that do not reflect skill, such as logging in.
- A system so steep that most users never reach the second level.
- Taking progress away harshly, so one missed day undoes months of work.
- Levels that change nothing about what the user can do.

## Example

On Stack Overflow, people earn reputation when others vote up their questions and answers, and
reputation unlocks privileges. At 15 points a user can vote up, and at 125 they can vote down. At
2,000 their edits to other people's posts apply at once, and at 3,000 they can vote to close
questions. At 10,000 they get moderator tools. Each level reflects trust from the community, and each
one gives more responsibility for the site.
