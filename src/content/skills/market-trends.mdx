## Why it matters for a product engineer

A trend answers the question "why now?": why a product that was not possible or not needed before can
work today. Engineers often see technical trends first, because they use new models, APIs and tools
every day. A trend is also a risk: what it opens for you, it opens for everyone else.

## How to do it

1. Write down the changes you notice in your own work: a new API, a model that got cheaper, a new
   platform rule, a new habit.
2. For each one, ask what it makes newly possible and what it makes newly necessary.
3. Look for evidence outside your own feed: pricing pages, adoption numbers, job posts, the dates when
   a regulation starts.
4. Ask who adopts first. Early adopters accept a rough product; most people wait for a complete one.
5. Write your "why now" in two sentences, and what would prove it wrong.
6. Check it again every few months.

## Common mistakes

- Taking noise for a trend: many posts, few paying users.
- Building on a trend that a platform can add as a feature. Mamitsu's maker sees that risk in products
  built on the AI labs' models or for their agents.
- Being too early: the technology works, but the habit is not there yet.
- Following a trend with no real user need under it.

## Example

In 2023, commercial open source was rebuilding older SaaS products: Cal.com for Calendly, Documenso
for DocuSign. Papermark joined the wave early as the open-source alternative to DocSend, and its OSS
Friends page still lists the others. The trend brought attention. Revenue waited until Papermark
matched the features buyers expected. ([Papermark in In practice](/in-practice/papermark/))
