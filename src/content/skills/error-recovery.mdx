## Why it matters for a product engineer

Errors are where a product shows whether it respects people's time. Engineers often write error
messages at the last minute, and they decide what the system does after a failure. A good error is
prevented, or it explains what happened in plain words and offers a way out.

## How to do it

1. Prevent the likely mistakes first: constrain inputs, use good defaults, and offer undo for
   destructive actions.
2. Ask "Are you sure?" only for actions that are hard to undo. NN/g warns that frequent confirmations
   teach people to click through without reading. Label the buttons with the action, such as
   "Delete file" and "Keep file".
3. Show the message next to the problem, after the user has acted, not while they are still typing.
4. Say what happened and how to fix it, in plain words. Put the blame on the system, not the user.
5. Keep what the user entered, and make the fix one step.
6. Score your messages with NN/g's rubric: twelve guidelines in three groups, visibility,
   communication and efficiency.

## Common mistakes

- Error codes and technical words in the message.
- Messages that blame the user, or sound like a reprimand.
- Errors shown at the top of the page, far from the field that caused them.
- Silent failures: nothing happens, and nothing says why.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp found silent misses. In the pain scene the agent pressed above the
point for seven seconds; nothing moved and nothing said why. In a session for someone in pain, a
silent miss feels like the person failed. ([RelievApp in In practice](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
