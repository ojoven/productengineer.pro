## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every change affects someone: users, customers, partners, other teams, leadership. They need the right
information at the right time, in a form they can use. Bad news shared early is usually accepted.
Surprises are not: they make people doubt everything else you say. Most stakeholder problems come
from silence, not from disagreement. Short, regular updates and early conversations about big changes
prevent most of them.

## How to do it

1. List who is affected by your work, and what each of them needs to know.
2. Talk to the most affected people before a decision is final, not after.
3. Send short, regular updates, including what is late or at risk.
4. Share bad news as soon as you know it, with what you plan to do.
5. When you get it wrong, say so plainly and change the plan.

## Common mistakes

- Announcing a big change without testing it on the people it affects.
- Updates that only report good news.
- Telling every stakeholder everything, so the important part gets lost.
- Correcting a mistake with more announcements instead of a conversation.

## Example

On 12 September 2023, Unity announced a new runtime fee: game developers would pay up to $0.20 per
install, from January 2024. The reaction was severe, and Unity clarified the rules in the following
days. On 22 September, Marc Whitten wrote: "I am sorry. We should have spoken with more of you."
A year later, Unity cancelled the fee after what its CEO, Matt Bromberg, called deep consultation with
its community, and raised subscription prices instead. He wrote that Unity "can't pursue that mission
in conflict with our customers".
