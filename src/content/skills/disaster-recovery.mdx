## Why it matters for a product engineer

Replication protects against a failed machine. It does not protect against a deleted account, a bad
migration or ransomware, because the mistake is copied too. Backups do, but only if they are separate,
complete and restorable. A recovery plan sets two numbers: how much data you can lose (the recovery
point objective, RPO) and how long you can be down (the recovery time objective, RTO). Product
engineers own the data and the code that restores it.

## How to do it

1. Set the RPO and RTO for each kind of data, with the people who know what a loss costs.
2. Keep backups outside the system they protect: another account, region or provider.
3. Restore from backup on a schedule, into a clean environment, and time it.
4. Write the restore steps as a runbook or a script that someone else can follow.
5. Monitor backups: alert when one fails, is late, or is much smaller than usual.

## Common mistakes

- Treating replicas as backups.
- Backups in the same account, so one mistake or attack deletes both.
- Backups that nobody has restored, so nobody knows whether they work.
- A recovery time that depends on one person who knows the steps.

## Example

In May 2024, Google Cloud deleted a private cloud of UniSuper, an Australian pension fund. When Google
operators had set it up a year earlier with an internal tool, they left one parameter blank. The system
gave it a default term of one year, and at the end of the year it deleted the private cloud, which ran
across two zones, with no notice. Backups in Google Cloud Storage were not affected. Together with
third-party backup software, they made the restore possible, after several days of work.
