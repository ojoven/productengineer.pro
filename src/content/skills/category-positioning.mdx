## Why it matters for a product engineer

The category you claim decides what people compare you with, which features they expect, and what
they search for. Engineers feel it directly: a category brings a list of features buyers take for
granted. Choose a category where your strengths are visible, and know which basics it makes
mandatory.

## How to do it

1. List the categories a buyer could put the product in, starting from what they use today.
2. For each one, write what buyers expect and who they would compare you with.
3. Choose the category where your strengths matter most and your gaps matter least. Often that is a
   sub-category of an existing market, such as "open-source data rooms".
4. Check that people search for it and understand the name.
5. Match the product to it: the basics the category demands, and the words on your pages.
6. Move to another category when the buyers change, and change every page at once.

## Common mistakes

- Inventing a new category nobody searches for.
- Claiming a category whose basic features you lack.
- A category that puts you next to a much bigger, better-known product.
- Different categories on different pages.

## Example

Papermark started as "the open-source DocSend alternative" and, as its buyers changed, became "secure
data rooms for dealmakers", with open source below the fold. RelievApp started in a category of its
own, transitions, which nobody searched for. It then tried meditation, and on 18 November became
"your personal emotional regulator". ([Papermark](/in-practice/papermark/),
[RelievApp](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
