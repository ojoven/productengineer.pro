## Why it matters for a product engineer

Messaging is the words people read before they decide to try the product: a headline, a page title, a
line in an app store. If the words are not the ones people use, they do not find the product, or they
arrive expecting something else. Product engineers ship these words in pages, emails and screens, and
they can see in analytics and search data whether the words work.

## How to do it

1. Collect the words customers use, from interviews, reviews, support tickets and search queries.
2. Start from positioning: who the product is for, what they use today and what is better. Then write
   the words.
3. Use the customer's term for the problem, even when the internal name is different.
4. Test titles and headlines with people who do not know the product: ask what they expect to find.
5. After a change, watch page views, search terms and feedback for signs that people arrive at the
   wrong place.

## Common mistakes

- Internal names and jargon in headlines.
- Clever words that nobody would search for.
- A message that promises more than the product does today.
- Changing the words without checking the analytics afterwards.

## Example

GOV.UK had a guide called "Exchange your tax disc", for drivers whose vehicle changes, such as a new
fuel type, affect their tax. In October 2014 the tax disc was scrapped, and the guide was renamed
"Change your vehicle tax". The vague title brought up to 10 times as many views as before: up to 3,000
people a day reached content they did not need. The team renamed it "Change your vehicle's tax class",
rewrote it, and decided to test titles with people in future.
