## Why it matters for a product engineer

Most products grow through one or two main channels. The right channel depends on where the buyers
are and on what the product can do, so it is not only a marketing choice. A channel can be built into
the product, such as a shared link or an integration, and engineers build those. A great product in
the wrong channel grows slowly.

## How to do it

1. Write where your target users already look for a solution: search, a marketplace, a community,
   another product they use every day.
2. List channels that fit both that place and your price. A cheap product cannot pay for sales calls.
3. Test several cheaply and for a short time, with the same success measure for each.
4. Keep one or two, and invest in them until they stop growing.
5. Look again when the buyer changes. The channel that found your first users rarely finds the next
   ones.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing channels you like rather than channels your buyers use.
- Spreading a small budget over many channels, so none gets enough to show a result.
- Judging a channel by visits instead of paying customers.
- Keeping the launch channel after the audience has moved.

## Example

Papermark's founders tried more than 14 channels and kept three. Their first users came from Twitter
and GitHub; today's buyers are deal teams on LinkedIn. RelievApp gave each channel its own front door,
a toy for TikTok and a reset for Reels, then folded them into one the next day.
