## Why it matters for a product engineer

A research report is a summary of a summary. Engineers who watch users struggle with a product
understand the problem in a way that a slide cannot give them. They also notice things a researcher
may miss: a slow page, a browser bug, a workaround that points to a missing feature. Researchers bring
method: good questions, fair recruitment and careful analysis. When the two work together, and analyse
the findings together, the team acts on research instead of filing it.

## How to do it

1. Ask the researcher what the study wants to learn, and add your technical questions early.
2. Observe sessions live, and take notes as a second observer.
3. Write down what users do and say, not your interpretation, and keep quotes exact.
4. Join the group analysis, and bring what you know about the system.
5. Turn findings into changes you can ship, and tell the researcher what happened.

## Common mistakes

- Reading only the final report, weeks after the sessions.
- Asking leading questions, or explaining the product during a session.
- Using one user's opinion to win an argument.
- Treating research as an internal agency that delivers answers to order.

## Example

In 2015 the GOV.UK service manual team did its discovery research as a whole team. In three weeks,
the 8 team members spoke to 29 people in 7 cities. Before leading an interview, each person first
observed a session and took notes. The team then analysed the 29 transcripts together. They planned one
day for the analysis and needed two. The researcher, Patricia Bartholomeou, wrote that this stopped
research being used "like an internal agency": everyone on the team had met users and could explain
their needs.
