## Why it matters for a product engineer

Sketching is the fastest way to explore layouts and flows. Its value is in quantity: several rough
ideas beat one polished idea, because you compare instead of defending. Engineers who sketch before
they code often find a simpler solution, and they throw away paper, not a branch.

## How to do it

1. Set a short time, such as ten minutes, and draw several different answers to one question.
2. Keep them rough: boxes, arrows and real words for the important labels. No colour, no pixels.
3. Make the ideas really different, not small variations of the first one.
4. Put them side by side and compare them against the user's task, not against taste.
5. Show the best two or three to someone else, or to a user, and watch where they get confused.
6. Only then move to a wireframe or code.

## Common mistakes

- One sketch, polished until it feels like a decision.
- Sketches so detailed that people comment on colours instead of the flow.
- Drawing only screens, and forgetting the steps between them.
- Skipping sketching because "coding is faster". For one idea, perhaps; for ten, it is not.

## Example

When Lego explored the Nexo Knights theme, designers made paper prototypes and showed them to small
children. The children's first reaction: "Hey, who are the bad guys? I can't see who's good and who's
bad!" The designers kept changing and testing the designs until children could tell.
