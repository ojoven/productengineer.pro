## Why it matters for a product engineer

Visual hierarchy uses size, weight, colour and position to guide the eye. A screen with no clear
hierarchy feels busy even when there is little on it. For engineers who build interfaces without a
designer, it is the most useful visual skill: most of it is a few CSS properties applied with intent.

## How to do it

1. Decide the one thing each screen is for, and make it the most noticeable element.
2. Rank everything else: primary, secondary, the rest. Use size, weight and contrast to show the rank,
   and use few levels.
3. Put related things together and the most important at the top, where people start reading.
4. Remove or merge elements that say the same thing.
5. Do the squint test: blur your eyes, or the screenshot. What stands out should be what matters.
6. Check that the action is next to the thing it changes, so the eye does not have to travel.

## Common mistakes

- Everything bold, large or coloured, so nothing stands out.
- Several texts that repeat one message.
- The main action styled like a secondary one.
- The control in one place and its effect in another, far away.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp counted five texts at the start of the breathing scene, and three said
the same thing. Some sat on top of the sphere that the session is about. The control was at the
bottom and the change at the top, so people watched the control, not the payoff. The audit's rule for
every scene: two texts at most, and none over the subject.
