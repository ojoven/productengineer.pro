## Why it matters for a product engineer

People build habits around a product, even around its flaws. A change that is better on paper still
breaks those habits, and the first reaction is often negative. Some of that reaction fades in a few
weeks. Some of it is a real signal that the change is wrong. Product engineers can tell the two apart
only if the change is released in a way that can be measured, limited and undone.

## How to do it

1. Write down why you make the change and what result you expect, before you build it.
2. Release it to a small group first, behind a flag that can switch it off.
3. Watch two kinds of evidence: what people say, and what they do.
4. Tell people before the change, say why, and give a way back or a transition period where you can.
5. Decide in advance which result means continue, adjust, or roll back.

## Common mistakes

- Large redesigns released to everyone on the same day.
- Reading all complaints as resistance to change, or all of them as proof of failure.
- No way to switch the old version back on.
- Explaining the change only after people complain.

## Example

In mid-2022, Instagram tested a full-screen home feed with a small percentage of users. Some of them
really disliked it. Kim Kardashian and Kylie Jenner shared a petition that said "Stop trying to be TikTok".
On 28 July, Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, said: "people are frustrated and the usage data isn't
great". Instagram phased out the test within one to two weeks, and also reduced recommended posts for
a time.
