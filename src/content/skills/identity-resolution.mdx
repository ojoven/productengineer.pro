## Why it matters for a product engineer

People browse anonymously, sign up, then come back on another device. If analytics sees three
people instead of one, user counts grow and funnels break, often at the signup step. Identity is
also a privacy decision: linking activity across devices is something people should know about. A
product engineer owns both sides.

## How to do it

1. Give each visitor an anonymous ID on the first visit.
2. At signup or sign-in, call identify with a stable user ID from your database, never an email or
   a name. The anonymous events then join that person.
3. Send server events with the same user ID, so browser and server agree.
4. Reset the identity at sign-out, so the next person on a shared device does not join the
   previous one.
5. Decide what you will not link: devices, households, accounts that share a login. Write it down.
6. Test it: sign up in a fresh browser, sign in on a second one, and check that one person appears.

## Common mistakes

- Calling identify with a value that is not unique, such as "guest" or "undefined".
- Different user IDs on the client and the server.
- Never resetting, so everyone on a shared computer becomes one person.
- Linking identities in ways the privacy notice does not mention.

## Example

This site's analytics stay anonymous on purpose. A campaign label, such as a newsletter link, stays
attached for 30 minutes in one browser tab, so an internal click does not erase it. Nothing links
visits across days or devices. So the analytics cannot show whether the same person came back in a
second week, which is the product's real goal. That evidence comes from observed sessions and
follow-ups instead.
