## Why it matters for a product engineer

A funnel shows where people leave. A session replay shows what they did just before: the
hesitation, the wrong click, the error message. For a product engineer, a few replays can turn a
drop-off into a bug you can reproduce. But a replay records whatever the page shows, so the privacy
settings are part of the setup, not an extra.

## How to do it

1. Start from a question, such as one drop-off step, and filter replays to the sessions that reached
   it.
2. Watch several sessions before you conclude anything. One session is a story, not a pattern.
3. Mask all input by default. Also mask or block page text that shows personal data, such as account
   and payment pages.
4. Test the masking on your own pages: record a session with fake data, and watch it.
5. Write what you saw as a bug or a finding, with links to the sessions. Delete recordings you do
   not need.

## Common mistakes

- Watching random sessions without a question.
- Trusting the tool's defaults to hide everything personal.
- Recording payment or health pages at all.
- Sharing replays widely inside the company.

## Example

In 2017, Princeton researchers studied session replay scripts that they found on 482 of the top
50,000 websites. Redaction of typed input was partial: one service hid card numbers only in fields
marked with a specific attribute. No service redacted text shown on the page by default, and the
account page of the clothing store Bonobos sent full card details to a replay service. Today PostHog
masks input fields by default, but it still does not mask page text by default.
