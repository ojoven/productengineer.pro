## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every field and every step before value loses some people. Signup is also where product and
engineering meet: what the account model needs, which sign-in methods you support, what you store.
A product engineer who can delay or simplify account creation often gains more than any new feature
would.

## How to do it

1. Ask what the user gets before the account, and what the account adds. Let people reach value
   first when you can.
2. Offer a path without an account, such as guest checkout, and make it as visible as sign-in.
3. Ask for the minimum: often an email address, or sign-in with an account people already have.
4. Offer to create an account after the value, for example on the confirmation page, as Baymard
   recommends.
5. Plan for returning users who forget which email they used: magic links, clear errors, account
   lookup.
6. Measure drop-off at each signup step.

## Common mistakes

- A login wall before people can see anything.
- Strict password rules that make people stop.
- Treating duplicate accounts as the user's problem.
- Asking for data the product does not need yet.

## Example

Jared Spool describes a large retailer whose checkout started with a form: Login or Register. In
usability tests, first-time buyers resented it. One said: "I'm not here to enter into a
relationship. I just want to buy something." Returning buyers could not remember their details: 45%
of customers had more than one account, and about 160,000 people a day asked for their password. The
team replaced Register with Continue, and a message: you do not need an account to buy.
