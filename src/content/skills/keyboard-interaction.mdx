## Why it matters for a product engineer

Many people use a keyboard instead of a mouse: screen reader users, people who cannot use a pointing
device, people whose mouse just stopped working, and people who are simply faster with keys. A page is
only accessible if everything works from the keyboard. Keyboard failures are common, and they are among
the easiest to test: put the mouse away and try.

## How to do it

1. Use native elements: a `button` for actions, an `a` with `href` for navigation. They are focusable
   and work with Enter and Space by default.
2. Keep the focus order logical by keeping the HTML in a logical order. Do not reorder it with a
   positive `tabindex`.
3. Show focus clearly on every interactive element. Never remove the outline without a better one.
4. If you must build a custom control, make it focusable with `tabindex="0"` and handle the keys people
   expect, the same way you handle clicks.
5. In dialogs and menus, move focus in when they open, keep it there, close with Escape, and return
   focus to where it was.
6. Test every new flow with the keyboard only, before release.

## Common mistakes

- A `div` with a click handler and nothing else.
- Focus outlines removed for looks.
- Keyboard traps, such as a widget that focus enters but cannot leave.
- Actions that appear only on hover.

## Example

WebAIM's 2026 scan found 4.8 elements with `role="button"` per home page, up from 3.6 a year before.
Each one is a control that was not built as a `button`. To work from the keyboard, each needs focus,
Enter and Space handling and a visible focus style: the things a native `button` has for free.
