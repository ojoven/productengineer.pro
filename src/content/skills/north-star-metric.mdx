## Why it matters for a product engineer

A North Star metric tells the whole team what "better" means. When engineers know which number shows
that users get value, they can judge their own work by it, and choose between two tasks without a
meeting. It also needs events that the engineers must define and keep correct.

## How to do it

1. Write the core value in one sentence: what users get when the product works.
2. Find the action that shows that value happened, such as a document viewed, a lesson finished or a
   message delivered. Count it over a period.
3. Check that it predicts what the business needs: people who do more of it stay longer and pay more.
4. Break it into three to five input metrics a team can move, such as new users who reach the action,
   or the action's frequency.
5. Add counter-metrics, because any single number can be gamed.
6. Make sure the events behind it are reliable, and that their definition is written down.

## Common mistakes

- Revenue as the North Star. It shows value late and says little about why.
- A number that grows without users getting value, such as page views or sign-ups.
- A metric no team can influence week to week.
- Changing it every quarter.
- An unclear definition, so two dashboards show two different numbers.

## Example

Papermark's North Star is the number of views of shared documents: 800,000 by mid-2025. A view means
that someone sent a document and someone else read it, which is the value for both sides. In the code,
every page a visitor views becomes one `page_views` row, with the time spent on it.
