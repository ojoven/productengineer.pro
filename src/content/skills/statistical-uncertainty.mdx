## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every metric moves a little every day, with no cause at all. If you do not know how much it usually
moves, you cannot tell a real change from noise, and every dip starts an investigation. A range is
more honest than a single number, and it protects a product engineer from building on noise.

## How to do it

1. Measure the normal variation first. Look at the metric over several weeks with no changes, or run
   an A/A test, where both groups get the same experience.
2. Report a range with every estimate, such as a confidence interval.
3. Remember that the difference between two numbers is noisier than each number on its own.
4. Expect segments to be noisier than the total, because they contain fewer people.
5. Look for several moves in the same direction before you call something a trend.

## Common mistakes

- Reacting to one day's dip.
- Reporting "up 3%" without a range.
- Finding big differences between small segments.
- Using the margin of one estimate as the margin of a difference.

## Example

Pew Research Center explains this with election polls. A random sample of 1,067 people has a margin
of error of about 3 points for one candidate's support. In Pew's example, the margin for the lead
between two candidates is about 6 points. For a change in the lead between two polls, it is 8
points, so a 3-point shift could be noise. In a subgroup of about 160 people, the margin for the lead
is 16 points.
