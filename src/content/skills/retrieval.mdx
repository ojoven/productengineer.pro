## Why it matters for a product engineer

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) finds the documents that matter and puts them in the model's
context, so it can answer from your data instead of its memory. The quality of the answer depends
mostly on the quality of the search. That makes RAG a search problem, which engineers can measure and
improve, and a permission problem, because a model can only keep secret what it never sees.

## How to do it

1. Apply access rules before search, so the model only sees documents the user may read.
2. Split documents into chunks that make sense alone, and add a short context to each one.
3. Combine search methods: embeddings for meaning, keyword search for exact terms and names.
4. Rerank the top results before they go into the prompt.
5. Evaluate retrieval on its own: for real questions, did the right passage come back?
6. Ask for answers with citations, and let the model say when the documents do not answer.

## Common mistakes

- Judging only the final answers, so you cannot tell if search or generation failed.
- Chunks cut in the middle of a table or a sentence.
- Filtering by permission after search, when the model may already have seen the text.
- Old documents that stay in the index after they change.

## Example

Papermark's data room assistant answers questions across a deal's documents, with citations back to
pages, and "only from documents the token is allowed to read". In a data room, the permission check
before retrieval matters as much as the answer. ([Papermark in In practice](/in-practice/papermark/))
