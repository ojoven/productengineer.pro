## Why it matters for a product engineer

Most of an interface is text. Font, size, line spacing and line length decide whether it is easy to
read. These are a handful of CSS values, and good defaults make an interface look deliberate before
any other styling.

## How to do it

1. Set body text first. Matthew Butterick names four choices that matter most: size, line spacing,
   line length and font.
2. Use 15 to 25 pixels for body text on the web, and line spacing of 120 to 145% of the size.
3. Keep lines between 45 and 90 characters, with a maximum width in `ch` or `rem`.
4. Choose one well-made font family, and use few sizes: body, a small size, and two or three for
   headings.
5. Use bold and italic rarely, and not together. Never underline anything except links.
6. Check the text on a phone and on a large screen, in light and dark themes.

## Common mistakes

- Body text below 15 px to fit more on the screen.
- Lines that run across a wide monitor.
- Many sizes that differ only a little, so the hierarchy is unclear.
- Light grey text on white, which looks elegant and reads badly.

Butterick's range. A line holds about 88 characters on a laptop and about 49 on a phone, both inside
