## Why it matters for a product engineer

Attribution decides which channel gets credit for a customer, and so where the next euro goes. Every
model is a simplification, and privacy rules, blocked scripts and AI answers hide more of the path
each year. Engineers decide what is collected and how long it is kept. A simple, honest setup with
known gaps is worth more than a detailed one nobody can explain.

## How to do it

1. Tag every link you control with campaign labels, such as UTM parameters, using one naming scheme.
2. Choose a model and a lookback window, and write them down. Last click and data-driven models
   give different answers.
3. Keep only what you need: the labels and the origin of the referrer, not full URLs or identities.
4. Ask new customers how they heard about you, with a free-text answer. It finds podcasts, friends
   and AI answers that leave no trace.
5. Compare channels by what their users do later, not by visits.
6. Write down what your setup cannot see, and do not read the gaps as "direct" traffic.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the model's answer as the truth about cause.
- Giving all credit to the last click, which favours search ads and brand searches.
- Tracking people across sites and devices without consent.
- Never asking customers, because the dashboard seems complete.

## Example

Mamitsu's maker can see Mamitsu cited more often in AI answers, but not whether those citations bring
users. People may read an answer and search for the name later. The cheapest next step is one question
at sign-up. This
site keeps campaign labels for 30 minutes in one browser tab, and no further.
([Mamitsu in In practice](/in-practice/mamitsu/))
