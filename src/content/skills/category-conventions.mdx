## Why it matters for a product engineer

People spend most of their time in other products, so they arrive with expectations. This is Jakob's
law. When a product follows the conventions of its category, people can start without learning
anything new. For an engineer, a convention is also the cheapest specification: the pattern exists,
millions of people have tested it, and users already know it. Break one only when the break is the
point.

## How to do it

1. List the products your users already use for this job, including the ones they dislike.
2. Note what they all do the same way: where settings live, how prices are shown, what the first
   screen asks, what things are called.
3. Mark each convention as follow or break, and write the reason for each break.
4. Follow the conventions where people only want to finish a task: sign-up, checkout, settings.
5. Break them where your difference is, and make the break visible and explained.
6. When you change a familiar pattern, let people keep the old version for a while. YouTube did this
   with its 2017 redesign: people could preview it, give feedback and go back.

## Common mistakes

- Breaking a convention by accident, because nobody looked at the category.
- Copying the market leader in everything, so nobody has a reason to switch.
- New names for familiar things.
- Forcing a redesign on everyone on the same day.

## Example

Papermark started as "the open-source DocSend alternative". Revenue waited until it matched the
features buyers expected, because "no one wants a half-baked product". It broke the convention on
price, with flat plans that include three users, and later named itself after its own buyer.
