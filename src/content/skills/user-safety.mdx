## Why it matters for a product engineer

Any product that connects people, shows content or knows where someone is can be used to hurt
someone. The person at risk is often not your user: it is the person being harassed, tracked or
defrauded. Safety features such as reporting, blocking, rate limits and alerts are product features.
They need design, testing and owners like any other feature. Thinking about misuse before launch is
much cheaper than reacting to it in the news.

## How to do it

1. Before launch, ask how a bad actor could use each feature, and who would be hurt.
2. Include the people who are not your users: the person being tracked, messaged or reviewed.
3. Build reporting, blocking and limits into the first version, not after the first incident.
4. Work with safety groups and law enforcement, who see patterns that you cannot.
5. Watch for misuse after launch, and keep improving the protections.

## Common mistakes

- Designing only for the intended use.
- Protecting only your own users, when the person at risk uses another platform.
- Treating safety as a moderation team's job, not a product decision.
- Waiting for a public incident before acting.

## Example

When Apple launched AirTag in April 2021, it included protection against unwanted tracking: iPhones
could warn their owners about an unknown AirTag travelling with them. People without an iPhone had less
protection: an AirTag away from its owner for an extended time would play a sound. In February 2022,
Apple said it had seen reports of misuse and announced more protections. In May 2024, Apple and Google
added alerts for unknown trackers to both iOS and Android, based on a shared specification.
