## Why it matters for a product engineer

Inclusive design considers the whole range of human ability and situation: a permanent disability, a
broken arm, a phone in bright sunlight. It treats accessibility as an input to design from the start,
not a fix at the end. Fixes at the end cost the most, and engineers are the ones who make them.

## How to do it

1. Include disability in your personas from the start: as separate personas, inside existing ones, or
   as a spectrum of permanent, temporary and situational limits.
2. Design for one specific person or case, then extend the solution to others.
3. Recruit disabled people for research and usability tests. Personas never replace testing.
4. Check every design for the basics before it reaches code: colour contrast, text size, target size,
   focus states and plain language.
5. Design every state of an action: default, focus, hover, active and disabled.
6. Write down the accessibility decisions next to the design, so the engineer builds them in.

## Common mistakes

- An accessibility review only at the end, when changes are expensive.
- Personas based on stereotypes or on real, identifiable people.
- Designing for "the average user", who does not exist.
- Treating accessibility as a separate project instead of part of every feature.

## Example

WebAIM tests the home pages of the top million websites every year. In 2026 the share with detectable
WCAG failures rose to 95.9%, ending six years of small improvements. Pages also grew, by 14.3% more
elements in one year. Accessibility checked at the end does not keep up with that growth; the most
common failure, low-contrast text, is decided in design.
