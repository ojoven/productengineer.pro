## Why it matters for a product engineer

Writing code, a query or a report is not the same as knowing that it is right. This was always true,
and AI makes it more important: it produces large amounts of work that looks correct. Some errors fail
loudly, such as a function that does not exist. Others run without errors and give a wrong result.
Product engineers are responsible for what ships, whoever or whatever wrote it. So "done" has to mean
"checked", with evidence that someone else can see.

## How to do it

1. Before you start, write what "correct" means: the requirement and a few cases with known answers.
2. Run the work. Do not trust a description of what it does, including your own.
3. Check against a source outside the work: real data, the original document, a second method.
4. Look hardest at the parts that would fail silently: numbers, edge cases, quotes and references.
5. Record what you checked and what you did not, so others know what is still open.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the code or text and judging it by how plausible it looks.
- Asking the same tool that produced the work whether it is right.
- Checking the easy parts and assuming the rest.
- Treating passing tests as proof when the tests do not cover the requirement.

## Example

On 1 March 2023, lawyers in a New York case against the airline Avianca filed a brief that cited court
decisions created by ChatGPT. The other side's lawyers could not find most of the cases. When doubts
grew, one of the lawyers asked ChatGPT whether one case was real. It said the case "does indeed exist".
The lawyers continued to stand by the cases until late May. On 22 June, the court fined them and their
firm $5,000.
