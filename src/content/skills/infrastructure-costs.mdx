## Why it matters for a product engineer

Infrastructure cost grows with usage, and with resources nobody remembers. The choices that drive it,
such as which provider, which database, how often a job runs, are made by engineers, usually without
seeing the bill. A product can grow fast and still lose money on every customer. Cost is a metric like
speed or errors, and the people who make the design choices should see it.

## How to do it

1. Put the monthly cost on a dashboard the team sees, split by service.
2. Find the main drivers: usually compute, storage, data transfer and paid third-party APIs.
3. Tag resources by team and feature, so every cost has an owner.
4. Remove what nobody uses: old environments, unused volumes, forgotten test servers.
5. Review the provider choice when the bill changes. A platform that was cheap at launch can be the
   most expensive part at scale.
6. Estimate the cost of a new feature before you build it.

## Common mistakes

- Nobody looking at the bill until it doubles.
- Paying for convenience long after the product has outgrown it.
- Optimising small costs while one large service grows unwatched.
- Moving providers for cost without counting the people and time the move takes.

## Example

37signals spent $3.2 million on cloud services in 2022, about $2.3 million of it on servers outside
file storage. It planned to buy about $600,000 of its own hardware and run it in rented racks, for a
yearly cost of about $840,000. Zernio is moving from Vercel to Cloudflare for the same reason: at its
scale, Vercel became very expensive. ([Zernio in In practice](/in-practice/zernio/))
