## Why it matters for a product engineer

Information architecture decides how features and content are grouped, named and connected. If people
cannot find a feature, for them it does not exist. Engineers turn the structure into routes, menus,
URLs and data models, so a weak structure costs twice: confused users and code that is hard to change.

## How to do it

1. List everything the product contains: pages, features, settings, types of content.
2. Ask users to group and name them (card sorting). Their groups often differ from the team's.
3. Choose one main way to organise: by task, by object, by audience or by question. Mixing several at
   the same level confuses people.
4. Name each group with the words users use, and use one word for one concept everywhere.
5. Test the structure without visual design (tree testing): give people a task and see where they
   look for it.
6. Turn it into stable URLs and navigation, and add links across groups for things that belong to
   more than one.

## Common mistakes

- Copying the company's org chart into the menu.
- Internal names that users do not know.
- Putting the same item in several places instead of linking to one.
- Deep menus with many levels.
- Changing URLs every time the structure changes.

## Example

This site's Skills Map has 350 skills in three levels: 12 territories, 72 capabilities, and the
skills. Each territory is grouped by the question it answers, such as "Can users understand, use and
benefit from the solution?", not by job title or tool. Each skill has one home and one URL that
follows the levels, and related skills link across territories.
