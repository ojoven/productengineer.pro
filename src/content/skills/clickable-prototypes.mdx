## Why it matters for a product engineer

A clickable prototype simulates a flow well enough to test it, without real logic. It answers whether
people understand the flow before anyone writes production code. For engineers, it is also a cheap
way to settle a debate: put both versions in front of five people and watch.

## How to do it

1. Write the question the prototype must answer, such as "Do people find the export option?".
2. Choose fidelity for that question on three separate scales: interactivity, visuals and content.
3. Make only the paths the test needs clickable, and use real content where people read it.
4. Test with a few real users and a task, and watch what they do, not what they say.
5. Change the prototype between sessions when a problem is clear.
6. Keep it apart from production code, so it stays quick to change and nobody ships it by accident.

## Common mistakes

- A prototype so polished that the team cannot bear to throw it away.
- Fake content, such as lorem ipsum, where the test depends on reading.
- Testing a prototype that only covers the happy path, then trusting the result for every path.
- Asking "Do you like it?" instead of giving a task.

## Example

GOV.UK requires service teams to make prototypes in the alpha phase, to explore and test different
designs before they commit to building anything. Teams across government use the GOV.UK Prototype Kit,
which makes realistic, interactive prototypes from the same components as the real services. The
guidance is to make several, test them, and discard the ones that do not test well.
