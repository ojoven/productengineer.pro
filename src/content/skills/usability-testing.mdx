## Why it matters for a product engineer

What the team finds obvious is often not obvious to a new user. A usability test shows you where
people get stuck, in their own hands, in an hour. For an engineer, a few sessions often explain a
drop in a funnel better than another week of analytics, and show which fix is worth building.

## How to do it

1. Choose two or three tasks that matter, such as "share this document with an investor". Write them
   as goals, not as instructions that name the buttons.
2. Recruit people who match your users. Colleagues and friends know too much.
3. Ask them to think aloud while they work. Stay quiet. Do not help, even when it hurts.
4. Note where they hesitate, go back, misread a label or give up, and what they say at that moment.
5. After each session, write the problems you saw, separate from your ideas for fixes.
6. Fix the biggest problems and test again with new people. Several small rounds teach more than one
   big study.

## Common mistakes

- Leading the participant: "Now click the blue button".
- Asking for opinions ("Do you like it?") instead of watching behaviour.
- Testing with people who already know the product.
- Waiting for a finished product before the first test.
- Writing a long report nobody reads, instead of fixing the top problems.
