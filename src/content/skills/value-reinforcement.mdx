## Why it matters for a product engineer

People stop noticing value they receive every day. Showing progress, time saved or results reached
helps them see what the product already does for them. It works when the value is real, and it fails
when the numbers are inflated. A product engineer owns the data behind these summaries, so they can
make sure the numbers are true.

## How to do it

1. Choose one number that reflects real value for the user: progress, time saved, results.
2. Show it at a natural moment, such as the end of a task, a week or a milestone.
3. Make early progress visible. Small numbers grow fast, and that motivates.
4. Compute it from real events, and say how it was calculated if it is not obvious.
5. Test it: compare retention for users who see it with users who do not.

## Common mistakes

- Vanity numbers, such as "you opened the app 40 times".
- Inflated estimates of time or money saved.
- The same summary for every user, whatever they did.
- Celebrating so often that it means nothing.

## Example

Duolingo shows a learner's streak, the number of days in a row they have practised. Its team notes
that early days feel the biggest: going from 2 to 3 days is a 50% increase, while going from 200 to
201 is 0.5%. So the screen after a lesson celebrates the streak, with special animations on some
milestone days. Seeing those animations made a brand-new learner 1.7% more likely to still use
Duolingo 7 days later.
