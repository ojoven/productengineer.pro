## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every customer who leaves has to be replaced before the product can grow. Churn analysis finds who
leaves, when, and after what, so a team can fix the causes it controls. Part of churn is not a choice
at all: a card expires and the payment fails. That part is an engineering problem, and often the
easiest to reduce.

## How to do it

1. Separate churn that customers choose from churn caused by failed payments. They need different
   fixes.
2. Measure churn per segment and per cohort. A blended rate mixes groups that behave very differently.
3. Look at what churned customers did before they left: falling usage, a feature they never used, a
   support ticket.
4. Ask at cancellation, with one short question and a free-text field.
5. Compare churned customers with similar ones who stayed, so the cause is not only a guess.
6. For failed payments, add smart retries, card updates and clear emails before the account closes.

## Common mistakes

- One blended churn rate for all customers.
- Trusting cancellation reasons alone. "Too expensive" often means "not enough value".
- Counting a failed card as a lost customer, with no retry.
- Fighting churn that is natural, such as a project that has ended.

## Example

Zernio's churn fell from 15 or 16% to 9%, as its founder gave it. Split by kind of customer, it is
about 2% for integrators, the developers who build Zernio into their products, and about 17% for end
users who post to their own accounts. The fix for one group is not the fix for the other.
([Zernio in In practice](/in-practice/zernio/))
