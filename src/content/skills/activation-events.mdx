## Why it matters for a product engineer

An activation metric turns "improve onboarding" into a number a team can move. It tells you which
events to track first and which step of the first session deserves engineering time. Without one,
teams improve signups, which are easy to move and say little about whether people stay.

## How to do it

1. Start from retention. Take users who are still active after one to three months and list what
   they did in their first days that users who left did not.
2. Write five to ten candidate definitions: one key action, the same action several times, or a
   combination of actions.
3. Give each one a time window, such as 7 or 30 days after signup, so the rate means the same thing
   every month.
4. For each candidate, compare how many users reach it with how many of those users stay. Choose the
   one with the best balance, not the one with the highest retention.
5. Record activation as an event in the product, not only in a query. Then the product can react,
   for example with a message to people who started but did not activate.
6. Check the definition again after a big product change. What predicted retention two years ago
   may not predict it today.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing signup or "opened the dashboard". It is too easy and predicts little.
- A definition so strict that only 1% of users reach it, even if all of them stay.
- No time window, so the rate drifts as old users activate late.
- Assuming the action causes retention. It may only show who was already engaged. Test changes with
  an experiment before you push everyone toward it.
- One definition for a product with very different uses.

## Example

PostHog tracks activation separately for each of its products. For session replay, an organisation
counts as activated when it analyses five recordings and sets a filter within 30 days of showing
interest. It counts as retained if it still analyses recordings three months later. The team writes
5 to 10 candidate definitions and compares activation and retention for each before choosing one.
