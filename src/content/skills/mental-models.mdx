## Why it matters for a product engineer

People act on what they believe a product does, not on how it works. When the interface matches that
belief, the product feels obvious. When the implementation leaks through, such as a sync state or an
internal status, people get confused and make mistakes. Engineers decide much of what leaks.

## How to do it

1. Learn what people already believe, from interviews, usability tests and the products they use
   every day.
2. Watch for the moment people hesitate or say "I thought it would…". That is a gap between their
   model and the product.
3. Teach one model and keep it the same everywhere: on the landing page, in the product and in help.
4. Hide the parts of the implementation people do not need, such as queues, retries and internal
   states. Show only what changes their decisions.
5. When the product must work differently from what people expect, say so where it matters, at the
   moment they need it.

## Common mistakes

- Designing from the database or the architecture, so the interface mirrors the code.
- Teaching one thing on the landing page and doing another in the product.
- Assuming people will read an explanation before they act.
- Changing a familiar pattern without a reason users would agree with.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp found that the front door taught one breathing gesture and the session
used another. People learned to press and hold, then met a handle on a track. Worse, the change they
came for happened at the top of the screen while the control was at the bottom. The audit's answer was
one breathing gesture, on the sphere itself. ([RelievApp in In practice](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
