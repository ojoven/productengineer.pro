## Why it matters for a product engineer

As a product grows, so does its data: in more tables, more tools and more hands. Data governance
answers the basic questions: who owns each dataset, who may see it, where it is stored, and when it is
deleted. Engineers build the answers into the system, with permissions, regions and retention jobs.
Good answers reduce risk, storage costs and the time spent on every audit or customer questionnaire.

## How to do it

1. List the data you hold, where it lives, and who owns each set.
2. Collect only what you need for a stated purpose, as the GDPR requires for personal data.
3. Give access by role, and log who reads sensitive data.
4. Set a retention period for each kind of data, and delete it automatically when the period ends.
5. Decide where data is stored, and let customers choose when their rules require it.
6. Review the list when you add a tool, a table or an integration.

## Common mistakes

- Keeping everything forever, "in case".
- Personal data copied into logs, analytics and test databases.
- Access given once and never removed.
- Nobody owning a dataset, so nobody notices when it goes wrong.

## Example

Papermark stores documents for deals, often with strict rules about where data may go. It hosts data
in Europe by default, offers other regions on enterprise plans, and remains open source, so a
regulated customer can run it on its own servers. ([Papermark in In practice](/in-practice/papermark/))
