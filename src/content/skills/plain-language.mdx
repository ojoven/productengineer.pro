## Why it matters for a product engineer

Interface text is part of the product. Plain language, with common words and short sentences, helps
everyone, including people reading in a second language and people under stress. Engineers often
write the first version of labels and error messages, and the words from the code are the ones that
leak into the interface.

## How to do it

1. Write for the reader's task, with the most important point first.
2. Use common words with one meaning. Prefer "start" to "initialise".
3. Keep sentences short, with one idea each, and use the active voice.
4. Use one word for one concept everywhere: in buttons, emails, help and errors.
5. Keep internal names out: the names of services, models and fields belong in the code.
6. Make rules checkable, for example a script that fails the build when a banned word appears.

## Common mistakes

- Technical words from the code in the interface.
- Several words for the same thing in different places.
- Idioms and wordplay that people reading in a second language cannot follow.
- Long sentences that hide the action.
- Thinking plain means childish. Plain writing can still be precise.

## Example

An audit of RelievApp on 16 November found that the sessions spoke like the code. The user saw "The
particles are following your rhythm now". The fix was a rule: no "field", "particles", "nucleus",
"membrane", "system" or "transition" in user copy, in English and Spanish, with a check that keeps them
out. The English is also written for people who learned it as a second language.
