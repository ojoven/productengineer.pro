## Why it matters for a product engineer

The most expensive way to learn what a product must do is to build it first. Doing the work by hand
for a few customers shows the steps, the exceptions and the value before anyone writes the automation.
Engineers then build what was proven, and they already know which parts are hard.

## How to do it

1. Choose which half you are testing. In a Wizard of Oz test the front end is built and a person does
   the back end. In a concierge test a person is the front end and uses whatever back end exists.
2. Start with a few real customers who have the problem now.
3. Do the work by hand, the same way each time, and write down every step and every exception.
4. Measure what you would measure in the product: did they come back, did they pay, did the result
   help?
5. Automate the steps that repeat. The exceptions you wrote down become your edge cases.
6. Decide what users are told. In a Wizard of Oz test they do not know a person is involved, so treat
   their data as carefully as the finished product would.

## Common mistakes

- Doing the work by hand for so long that it never becomes a product.
- Serving friends who would say yes to anything.
- Not writing the steps down, so the manual work teaches nothing.
- A manual service much better than the automation can ever be, so the results do not hold.

## Example

Aardvark answered questions by sending them to people who knew the answer. At first there was no
algorithm. The team posted each question to online users by hand to see who would answer, then pasted
the answer back to the person who asked. To users, it looked like a working system.
