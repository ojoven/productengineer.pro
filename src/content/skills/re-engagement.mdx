## Why it matters for a product engineer

Users who stopped are cheaper to bring back than new users are to find. They already know the
product and have set it up. But a message that brings them back to the same product that lost them
usually loses them again. Engineers build the triggers, the data behind each message and the state
that is waiting when the user returns.

## How to do it

1. Define "dormant" for your product's rhythm, and send the first message soon after usage drops.
2. Trigger messages from events, not from a calendar: something new for them, a task left half done,
   a change they asked for.
3. Show value they have missed, with their own data, not a generic "we miss you".
4. Make the return easy: saved work, the same settings, one click back to where they stopped.
5. Ask the ones who do not return why they left.
6. Measure what share of returning users are still active a month later, not only the clicks.

## Common mistakes

- Messages so late that the user has already moved on.
- Discounts as the first move, which teach people to leave and wait.
- Bringing users back to the problem that made them leave.
- Counting an open or a click as a user won back.

## Example

Papermark offers a three-month pause instead of a cancellation, and the links keep working. If someone
opens a paused team's data room, the team still gets an email about the visit. The details are held
back until they unpause. Each visit becomes a reason to return, at the moment a deal is active again.
