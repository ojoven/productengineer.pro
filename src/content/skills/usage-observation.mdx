## Why it matters for a product engineer

Analytics show what people do, not what they were trying to do. Sitting with someone while they use
the live product, in their own setting, shows the workaround, the second tab and the moment they give
up. It is the cheapest way to find uses you never designed for. Engineers who watch real use stop
arguing about edge cases: they have seen them.

## How to do it

1. Ask people to use the product for their own work, not for tasks you set. That is the difference
   from a usability test.
2. Watch where they really use it: at their desk, on their phone, or remotely with a shared screen.
3. Ask about what you see as it happens: "What were you looking for there?"
4. Note what they use alongside your product, such as spreadsheets, notes or another tool.
5. Write down what surprised you straight after the session, before you forget.
6. Compare what you saw with your analytics, and turn each surprise into a question for the data.

## Common mistakes

- Turning the session into a demo or a test with tasks.
- Explaining the product when they get stuck, so you never see where it fails.
- Watching only power users, or only friends.
- Sessions that end as a recording nobody watches.

## Example

This site's own goal is ten engineers who use a personal map in two separate weeks. Its analytics
are anonymous on purpose: they cannot tell whether two visits are the same person, and they never
see search terms. So observed sessions and follow-ups are part of the plan, not an extra. Mamitsu's
maker does the simplest version: now and then, he reads what people ask the product.
([Mamitsu in In practice](/in-practice/mamitsu/))
