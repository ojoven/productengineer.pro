## Why it matters for a product engineer

Behaviour shows what people do, not how they feel about it. Satisfaction metrics ask directly, and
catch frustration before it becomes churn. Scores are noisy and depend on who answers, so a product
engineer reads the trend and, above all, the written comments, which point at what to fix.

## How to do it

1. Choose a standard instrument and keep it: CSAT for one interaction, SUS for perceived usability,
   NPS for willingness to recommend.
2. Add one open question, such as "What problems or frustrations have you had?".
3. Sample on purpose: define the user segments you care about, and get enough answers in each one.
4. Do not ask the same person too often.
5. Code the comments into themes every cycle, and link the themes to work.
6. Read the score next to behaviour, such as retention. One does not replace the other.

## Common mistakes

- Asking only right after a success, when people feel best.
- Treating a small change in the score as real.
- Collecting comments and never reading them.
- Rewarding teams for the score, so they start managing who answers.

## Example

GitLab has measured usability with the System Usability Scale since 2019, as a key indicator for its
product teams. Ten standard statements are followed by one open question about problems and
frustrations. Each user is invited at most once a year, and each segment needs enough answers to
give a confident score. Every quarter, the team codes the answers into themes.
