## Why it matters for a product engineer

Users notice problems that dashboards miss: a confusing word, a missing option, a step that does not
fit their situation. Most of them will not say so unless it is easy and they believe someone will read
it. A product engineer can put the request in the right place, attach the context that makes feedback
useful, and send it to where the team works.

## How to do it

1. Offer a feedback link on every page, in the same place, so people know where to find it when they
   need it.
2. Say why you ask, what you will do with the answer, and how long it takes.
3. Keep feedback separate from support, so people with an urgent problem reach help.
4. Attach context automatically: page, app version, browser, recent errors. Do not ask users for data
   you already have.
5. Collect all feedback in one place, tag it, and tell users when their feedback led to a change.

## Common mistakes

- A pop-up survey in the middle of a task.
- A feedback link in a place people have learned to ignore.
- Forms with many required fields for a short comment.
- Feedback that stays in one person's inbox and is never reviewed.

## Example

GOV.UK services used to put the feedback link inside the phase banner, at the top of the page. Desk
research found that services struggled to get enough feedback that way. In August 2026, the GOV.UK
Design System introduced a separate Feedback component, placed just before the footer. Its heading and
message should say why the team wants feedback and what it will do with it. The component is in trial,
to learn whether it really brings more feedback.
