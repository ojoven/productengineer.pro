## Why it matters for a product engineer

Numbers show what is happening, users' words show why, and neither shows what the company should do.
A metric can point one way while interviews point the other. Product engineers often hold both
pieces: they see the data and they read the tickets. The decision still needs a view on strategy and
cost, made openly.

## How to do it

1. Write the decision first, and what would change your mind.
2. Collect the numbers: the main metric and the guardrails, the metrics that must not get worse.
3. Collect the qualitative side: interviews, tickets, sessions you watched.
4. When they disagree, look for the reason. Often each describes a different group of users.
5. Add what the data cannot show, such as word of mouth, brand or the cost of support.
6. Decide, and write down which signal won and why, so you can check later.

## Common mistakes

- Letting one metric decide because it is the easiest to defend.
- Dismissing a pattern in interviews because it is "anecdotal".
- Weighing the loudest users as if they were the most important.
- Deciding without recording the reasons.

## Example

On the Itnig podcast, Zernio's founder had just learned that developers building Zernio into their
products were 2% of signups and half the revenue. The host pushed for the obvious move. The founder
weighed what the revenue number does not show, and the host weighed the noise those users make.
