## Why it matters for a product engineer

A running experiment can go wrong in two ways. It can hurt users while the target metric improves.
Or it can be broken, so its numbers mean nothing. Guardrails catch both. The second kind is usually
a bug in assignment, logging or data processing, which is the product engineer's own area.

## How to do it

1. Choose guardrail metrics that must not get worse, such as revenue, errors, page speed and
   unsubscribes.
2. Decide how much harm is acceptable before the test. Spotify calls this the non-inferiority
   margin. For example, ship only if there is evidence that sales do not fall by more than 1%.
3. Check the sample ratio before anyone reads results. Compare the number of users in each group
   with the planned split, using a statistical test such as chi-square.
4. If the check fails, find the cause first. Look at segments, and at the condition that decides who
   is counted in the analysis.
5. Set the stopping rule in advance. Do not stop because one day's result looks good.

## Common mistakes

- Reading results before the sample ratio check.
- Judging the ratio by eye instead of testing it.
- Guardrails with no threshold, so every decision becomes a debate.
- Stopping early on a good day.

## Example

A team at MSN tested an image carousel with 16 cards instead of 12. The results showed lower
engagement, with a warning that the groups did not match the planned split. An investigation found
the cause. People who saw 16 cards engaged so much that a bot detection algorithm removed them from
the analysis. When the problem was fixed, the result reversed: the extra cards increased engagement.
