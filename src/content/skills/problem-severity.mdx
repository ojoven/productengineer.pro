## Why it matters for a product engineer

Teams often rank problems by who complained loudest, or last. Severity and frequency put every problem
on the same two scales. A severe, frequent problem deserves a real fix; a rare, mild one may deserve a
workaround, or nothing. For an engineer, the two scales also set how much effort and risk a fix is
worth.

## How to do it

1. Define the scales before you score. What does "severe" mean for your users: lost money, lost data,
   a missed deadline, distress?
2. Measure frequency from behaviour where you can: logs, support tickets, how often the situation
   happens.
3. Judge severity by what people already do about it. Ask what it costs them, and what else they have
   tried. If they have not looked for a solution, they probably will not buy yours.
4. Score each problem on both scales, and write down whether each score is measured or estimated.
5. Look at willingness to pay separately. A frequent problem can still be one nobody pays to solve.
6. Score again after research, so estimates become measurements.

## Common mistakes

- One combined score that hides which scale is high.
- Severity judged by the team, not by the people who have the problem.
- Frequency taken from the number of complaints, which counts the loudest people.
- Estimates presented as measurements.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp rated each state in the catalog by severity and frequency, and said that
the ratings were estimates. Two things followed. Chronic pain was the most severe and happened daily,
but had one session. And the free plan gave away a session for anxiety, the main job, so a person who
came for anxiety had little reason to pay.
