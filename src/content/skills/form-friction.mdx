## Why it matters for a product engineer

Each field costs effort, and some cost trust. Long forms, unclear labels, strict validation and early
questions about sensitive data all make people stop. A product engineer often owns the validation
rules and the data model, and those decide how many fields a form needs.

## How to do it

1. List every field and why the product needs it now. Remove or delay the rest.
2. Use defaults where you can, such as a billing address that equals the delivery address.
3. Use one field where people think of one thing, such as a full name.
4. Validate gently: accept common formats, and explain errors next to the field.
5. Hide optional fields, such as promotion codes, behind a link.
6. Measure drop-off and errors for each field.

## Common mistakes

- Separate fields that people fill in wrongly, such as first and last name.
- Rejecting spaces or dashes in phone and card numbers.
- Asking for a phone number without saying why.
- Showing every optional field by default.

## Example

Baymard Institute found that most e-commerce sites need only 8 form fields for checkout, but the
average checkout in 2024 had 11.3. Defaults explain part of the gap. In Baymard's tests, 42% of
participants typed their full name into a "First name" field at least once. On sites with one "Full
name" field, only 4% hesitated briefly. And 24% of sites show separate billing address fields by
default, a whole set of fields that most people do not need.
