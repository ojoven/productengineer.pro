## Why it matters for a product engineer

A feature nobody knows about has not really shipped. Release notes help users notice what changed
and why it matters to them, and they prepare support and sales for the questions. For a product
engineer they are also a test: if you cannot explain a change in two sentences of user language, the
change may not be clear yet.

## How to do it

1. Write for the user, not the commit log: what they can do now, and why it helps.
2. Lead with the benefit in one sentence, then the details.
3. Group small changes, and give the big ones their own entry.
4. Publish in more than one place: a public changelog, an email or in-product message for big changes,
   a note for the team before the release.
5. Link to the documentation, and show the feature where people will use it.
6. Date every entry, and keep old ones. The history is useful to customers and to you.

## Common mistakes

- Commit messages copied into the changelog.
- Notes full of internal names.
- Announcing only big launches, so steady improvement goes unseen.
- Release notes that appear after support has already had the questions.

## Example

Papermark ships almost every day, and its public changelog turns that into something customers can
follow. Each entry names the feature in user words, says what problem it solves, and lists what you
can do now. Small entries link to a monthly roundup, and the milestone posts list every release month
by month. ([Papermark in In practice](/in-practice/papermark/))
