## Why it matters for a product engineer

People give up when the effort is bigger than they expected. Saying how long a task takes, how many
steps it has and what to have ready keeps them going, and it reduces support requests. For a product
engineer, the promise must match the real behaviour: if an import takes ten minutes, the product must
say so and show progress.

## How to do it

1. Before a long task, say roughly how long it takes and what people need to have at hand.
2. Say what happens if they do not have it, so one missing detail does not stop them.
3. Show progress inside the task, such as "step 2 of 4".
4. For slow work, such as imports, give an estimate, and tell people when it is done.
5. Check the promises against real data, such as the median completion time, and update them.

## Common mistakes

- "It only takes a minute" for a task that takes ten.
- Asking for a document halfway through, with no warning at the start.
- Progress bars that do not move.
- Promises written once and never checked against the product.

## Example

GOV.UK's guidance says that a service's start page should say roughly how long the service takes and
what users need before they start. The Register to vote page does this in four short lines. It says
how often you need to register, how long it takes, and which number you will be asked for. It also
says that you can register without that number.
