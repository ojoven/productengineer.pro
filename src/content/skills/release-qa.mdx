## Why it matters for a product engineer

Automated tests check what someone thought to write down. Before a release, a product engineer also
checks the product the way people use it: the most important tasks, on real devices, with real data.
The goal is confidence in the release, not a perfect product.

## How to do it

1. List the critical paths: the few tasks people cannot live without, such as signing in, paying,
   or the product's core action.
2. Run the automated suite, then try each critical path by hand on the release build.
3. Spend a short, focused session on exploratory testing of what changed: unusual inputs, slow
   networks, old devices, other languages.
4. Check what a rewrite removes, not only what it adds. Compare its features with the old version,
   one by one.
5. Test with the setups real customers have, including large accounts and old data.
6. Decide to ship, ship to fewer people, or wait. Write down the known issues either way.

## Common mistakes

- Testing only the new screens, and not the old journeys around them.
- Testing with a clean demo account instead of real, messy data.
- A rewrite that ships with features missing, on a fixed date.
- Treating QA as a phase at the end, instead of a check on each change.

## Example

Sonos released a rebuilt app on 7 May 2024. On 25 July, its CEO wrote that "too many of you have
experienced significant problems" and apologized. Since the launch, the team had released updates
about every two weeks, and fixing the problems had delayed adding the missing features. The plan he
published for the next months was mostly the basics.
