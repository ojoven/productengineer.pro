## Why it matters for a product engineer

Much of SEO is product and engineering work: pages built from what the product knows, rendered so
that search engines can read them. It is also a channel that keeps working after the work stops. And
the pages that rank are often the pages that AI answers read and cite.

## How to do it

1. Start from the questions your users already type. Read the queries in Search Console and listen to
   the words customers use in sales and support calls.
2. Give each question one page that answers it better than the current results, with your product's
   own data or experience.
3. Link every page with normal `<a>` links, list it in a sitemap, and use URL Inspection to see how
   Google renders it.
4. Give each page a unique title and description and a readable URL. Point duplicates to one
   canonical URL.
5. Write honest comparison pages for the tools people leave to come to you.
6. Ask AI assistants the questions your users ask, regularly, and read which pages they cite.

## Common mistakes

- Pages produced in bulk with nothing new in them. Zernio's founder learned this on an earlier
  product: 50,000 AI-written posts, then Google removed the site from its index.
- A product name made of common words, which nobody can search for.
- Important content that appears only after scripts run in the browser, never checked in URL
  Inspection.
- Different facts about your product on different sites. AI answers trust a product less when the
  sources disagree.

## Example

About 40% of Zernio's new business comes from organic Google, on long-tail searches such as "social
media API" and "Meta app review". Ayrshare, the direct rival, owned those results; Zernio now ranks
above it on many. The product was renamed from Late partly for search: "Try searching 'Late API' and
you'll understand." ([Zernio in In practice](/in-practice/zernio/))
