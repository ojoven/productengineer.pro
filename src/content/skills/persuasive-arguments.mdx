## Why it matters for a product engineer

Product engineers often see a risk or an opportunity before others do. Seeing it is not enough: the
team has to agree to act. A persuasive argument states a clear claim, shows the evidence, and is honest
about the downsides. It also starts from what the other person cares about, which is rarely the
technical detail. The aim is a better decision. Sometimes that means being persuaded yourself, or
committing to a decision you argued against.

## How to do it

1. Start with the answer: what you recommend, in one sentence.
2. Give two or three reasons, each with its evidence.
3. Say what the option costs and what could go wrong.
4. Connect the argument to the other person's goals, not only yours.
5. Answer the strongest objection, not the weakest. If you lose, commit and move on.

## Common mistakes

- Burying the recommendation at the end of a long explanation.
- Arguing from authority or volume instead of evidence.
- Hiding the downsides, which makes people trust the rest less.
- Treating a disagreement as something to win.

## Example

In his 2016 letter to shareholders, Jeff Bezos described a decision about an Amazon Studios original.
He told the team his view: it might not be interesting enough, it was complicated to produce, the
business terms were not good, and there were other opportunities. The team disagreed and wanted to go
ahead. He replied at once that he disagreed and committed. His point: a candid view, weighed by the
team, then a fast decision, is better than a long fight to convince the boss.
