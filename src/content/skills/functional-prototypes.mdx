## Why it matters for a product engineer

Some ideas only make sense with real behaviour: real data, real delays, real AI output, real touch.
A functional prototype tests them with code that is meant to be thrown away. Product engineers can
build one in hours, and with coding agents often in minutes. The skill is less in building it and more
in learning from it and then letting it go.

## How to do it

1. Write the one question the prototype must answer, such as "does this gesture calm people?".
2. Build only what the question needs. Skip accounts, settings and edge cases.
3. Build several different versions when you do not know the answer, instead of one polished one.
4. Put it in front of people quickly, or send it instead of a long explanation.
5. Write down what you learned and what you decided, next to the prototype.
6. Keep the prototype separate from the product code. Take the idea forward, not the code.

## Common mistakes

- A prototype that grows into the product, with its shortcuts.
- Polishing it before anyone has seen it.
- One prototype, so there is nothing to compare.
- Showing it without a question, and collecting only compliments.

## Example

Unai Esteibar, who makes Mamitsu, often builds a two-hour demo and sends that instead of explaining
an idea; many ideas are built in the morning and thrown away by the afternoon. On 18 November an agent
built ten prototypes of interactive emotional regulation for RelievApp, from zero, in about 75
minutes. ([Mamitsu](/in-practice/mamitsu/), [RelievApp](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
