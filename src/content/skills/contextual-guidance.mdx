## Why it matters for a product engineer

Help is most useful at the moment someone needs it, next to the thing they are trying to use.
Contextual guidance puts a hint on an unfamiliar field, or a tip on the first use of a tool. It is
easy to overdo, because every tip interrupts someone. Each tip should solve a problem you have
observed.

## How to do it

1. Start from observed problems: support tickets, session replays, usability tests.
2. Choose the lightest form that works: better labels or hint text first, then a tooltip, then an
   overlay.
3. Trigger the help by what the user is doing, such as pointing at a new icon or starting a related
   task.
4. Keep it to one short sentence, and let people dismiss it.
5. Remove the tip when a design change fixes the problem.

## Common mistakes

- Tips at launch about things people are not doing.
- Guessing intent badly, so help appears at the wrong time.
- Information people need, hidden in a tooltip.
- Adding tips instead of fixing a confusing design.

## Example

NN/g calls help that appears out of context a push revelation, and help triggered by what the user
is doing a pull revelation. Figma used a pull revelation to explain a change to its default text
rendering: the tip appeared only when the user was doing something related. Photoshop shows a
tooltip with a short animation when someone points at a tool. Microsoft's Clippy tried to do this
with AI and failed: it was intrusive, and it often guessed wrongly what the user was doing.
