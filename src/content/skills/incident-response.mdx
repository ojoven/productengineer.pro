## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every product fails sometimes. What users remember is how long it lasted and how they were told.
Incident response is the plan for those hours: who leads, who fixes, who talks to users. Product
engineers are often the people closest to the failing code, so they need to know the plan before
they need it.

## How to do it

1. Decide in advance how an incident is declared, and that anyone may declare one.
2. Give clear roles: one person leads and decides, others fix, one person communicates.
3. Restore service first. Roll back, turn off a flag or move traffic, then look for the cause.
4. Keep a running log with times, actions and decisions, where everyone can read it.
5. Tell users early and regularly, even when you do not know the cause yet.
6. Write a blameless postmortem: what happened, why the system allowed it, and the actions that will
   stop it from happening again.

## Common mistakes

- Everyone fixing at once, with nobody in charge.
- Looking for the root cause while users are still down.
- Silence towards users until the fix is ready.
- Postmortems that blame a person, so the next mistake is hidden.
- Backups and recovery steps that nobody has tested.

## Example

In January 2017 an engineer at GitLab, working on a database problem, deleted data on the primary
database instead of the secondary. None of the backups worked. A snapshot taken six hours
earlier saved most of the data. GitLab worked in the open, with a public document and a live stream,
and published a blameless postmortem.
