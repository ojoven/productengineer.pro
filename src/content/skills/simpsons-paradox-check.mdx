## Why it matters for a product engineer

A total can move one way while every group inside it moves the other way, because the mix of groups
changed. After a launch, a campaign or a pricing change, the mix of users almost always changes. A
product engineer who checks key segments before reading the total avoids celebrating a change that
made things worse for everyone, or blaming one that helped.

## How to do it

1. Split every important result by the main segments: new and returning users, platform, country,
   plan.
2. Compare the mix of segments between the two groups or periods.
3. If the mix changed, compare within each segment, and weight the segments in the same way.
4. In an experiment, do not change the traffic split while it runs. Optimizely advises pausing and
   starting a new experiment instead.
5. Treat a win in one segment as a new hypothesis to test, not as a decision to ship.

## Common mistakes

- Changing a test's split from 99/1 to 50/50 halfway through.
- Comparing this month with last month after the user mix changed.
- Searching many segments until one shows a win.
- Reporting only the total.

## Example

Early in the Covid-19 pandemic, Italy's case fatality rate was 4.4%, almost twice China's 2.3%.
Researchers compared a large Chinese study from 17 February 2020 with Italian data from 9 March. In
every age group, Italy's rate was lower or the same. The difference came from the mix of cases: in
Italy, 18.4% of cases were people aged 80 or over, compared with 3.2% in China.
