## Why it matters for a product engineer

Completing one real task early builds confidence, and it shows the product's value better than any
tour. The task should be quick, common and satisfying. A product engineer can remove what stands in
the way: the account, the setup, the empty project, the wait.

## How to do it

1. Choose the first task: the smallest action that gives most users what they came for.
2. Remove every step before it that is not needed, including signup when you can.
3. Provide defaults or a template, so the task works with no configuration.
4. Show the result clearly, so people know that they succeeded.
5. Then ask for the next commitment, such as an account, to keep the result.
6. Measure the time from first visit to first success, and the share of new users who get there.

## Common mistakes

- A first task that only works after full setup.
- Celebrating a step that is not value, such as "profile completed".
- Asking for an account before people know what they will get.
- Reporting the average time, which hides the people who never finish.

## Example

Vercel lets developers deploy a project before they create an account, with `vercel deploy
--temporary` or by dropping a folder on the Vercel Drop page. The result is a live production URL,
shown with a Claim Project button and a countdown. Claiming moves the project into the developer's
account. If nobody claims it, the deployment expires and is gone. Vercel says this lets a developer,
or an AI agent, reach a live URL right away. Framework builds still need an account.
