## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every screen, field and confirmation between sign-up and value loses some people. Most of those
steps were added for a reason that made sense once, and nobody checked them again. Removing or
postponing them is often the cheapest activation work there is, and engineers can do much of it
alone: defaults, saved state, fewer fields.

## How to do it

1. Walk the flow as a new user, on a phone, and count every screen, field and tap before value.
2. For each step, ask what would break without it. If nothing, remove it.
3. Postpone what is needed later: ask for the account after the first value, and for details when
   they are used.
4. Replace typing with defaults, templates and data you already have.
5. Measure where people drop out before and after. One step removed can move more than a redesign.
6. Check the steps again after each release. They come back.

## Common mistakes

- Forced account creation before the person has seen anything.
- A form that asks for everything "in case".
- Counting screens and missing a slow one, such as an email to confirm.
- Merging steps into one long page and calling it shorter.

## Example

Papermark's data rooms start from a template for fundraising, M&A or reporting, or AI proposes the
folders from a description of the deal, so "the first hour goes into the deal, not into folders".
On staging, RelievApp's first session went from about seven screens before the first breath to three:
Start, one question, Continue. ([Papermark](/in-practice/papermark/), [RelievApp](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
