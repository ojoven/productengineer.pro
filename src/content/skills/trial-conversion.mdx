## Why it matters for a product engineer

A trial converts when people reach value before it ends. Trial length, card requirements and
reminders all shift the numbers, but the main lever is activation during the trial. A product
engineer can measure who reaches value, and when, and shape the trial around that.

## How to do it

1. Define activation for the trial: the action that predicts a purchase, within a fixed time window.
2. Report conversion separately for users who activated during the trial and users who did not.
3. Test the trial length instead of assuming that longer is better.
4. Test whether to ask for a card up front. It changes who signs up, not only how many.
5. Send reminders tied to progress, not only to the end date.
6. Look at segments: different users may need different trials.

## Common mistakes

- A long trial by default, to be generous.
- Counting trial signups as success.
- Reminders only on the last day.
- A paywall that blocks the action that shows the value.

## Example

A large company that sells a suite of software products ran a field experiment with 337,724 new users
in six markets. Each new user got a 7, 14 or 30-day free trial at random. A 7-day trial for everyone
increased subscriptions by 5.59% compared with the usual 30 days. A 14-day trial made no significant
difference. Choosing the length per user did better still. The users who did best with 7 days were
more often beginners and students.
