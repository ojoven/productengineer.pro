## Why it matters for a product engineer

Missing events, duplicates, bots and tracking that breaks in a release all change the numbers
without any error. People stop using data they do not trust, and then decisions go back to opinion.
A product engineer who tests tracking like code, and says openly when data was wrong, keeps the
data worth using.

## How to do it

1. Test tracking in CI: fire each important event in a test, and check its name and properties.
2. Validate events against their schema when they arrive, and count the ones rejected.
3. Monitor volumes per event. A sudden drop to zero or a jump after a release is a bug until proven
   otherwise.
4. Exclude internal users, test accounts and bots, and keep the rules for that in one place.
5. When data was wrong, publish a note: what was affected, the dates, the estimated impact. Mark it
   on the charts.
6. When you change a definition, say from which date it applies.

## Common mistakes

- Counting bot traffic as people.
- Duplicate events with different names for the same action.
- Fixing a tracking bug without marking the bad period.
- Comparing periods measured with different rules.

## Example

Around May 2025, Wikipedia saw unusually high traffic that looked human, mostly from Brazil. The team
investigated and updated its bot detection. With the new rules, it reclassified March to August, and
found that much of the May and June traffic came from bots built to avoid detection. After the
revision, human pageviews were about 8% lower than in the same months of 2024. The team also warned
that the old and new rules make comparisons over time harder.
