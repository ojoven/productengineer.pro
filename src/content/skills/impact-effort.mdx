## Why it matters for a product engineer

Comparing value with cost is the simplest way to choose what to build next. Engineers bring the most
honest part of it, the effort. The weak part is the impact, which is usually a guess. A good method
says so openly, instead of hiding the guess inside a precise-looking score.

## How to do it

1. List the candidate projects, each with the goal it serves.
2. Estimate reach from real data where you can: how many people or events per period.
3. Estimate impact per person on a small scale, and write the reason next to it.
4. Add your confidence in those estimates. Low confidence is a reason to test first, not to skip.
5. Estimate effort roughly, for the whole team, in person-weeks or person-months.
6. Rank by score, then read the list as a team. The score starts the discussion; it does not end it.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the score as a measurement. It is a structured guess.
- Precise effort and invented impact.
- Ignoring confidence, so exciting but vague ideas win.
- Scoring alone and presenting a finished ranking.
- Never checking afterwards whether the impact happened.

## Example

Intercom's product team created RICE because comparing ideas had become what Sean McBride called "a
tangled mess of gut feeling". Reach counts customers per quarter. Impact uses a fixed scale, from 3
for massive to 0.25 for minimal. Confidence is there "to curb enthusiasm for exciting but ill-defined
ideas". Effort, in person-months, divides the rest.
