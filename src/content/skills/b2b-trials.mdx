## Why it matters for a product engineer

A business customer wants proof that the product works with its data, its systems and its people
before it signs. A trial without agreed goals often ends with no decision: people try a few features,
then get busy. A proof of concept with success criteria turns the evaluation into a decision. Product
engineers often set up the environment and the integrations, so they see first whether the product
can meet the criteria.

## How to do it

1. Agree in writing on the problem, the use cases to prove and measurable success criteria.
2. Name the people on both sides, the environment, the end date and who decides.
3. Prepare the environment before the start, and run a smoke test, so day one is not spent on access.
4. Check progress against the criteria halfway, and change the plan only with the customer's
   agreement.
5. Close with a readout and a recorded result: win, loss or no decision, with the reasons.

## Common mistakes

- An open sandbox with no success criteria and no end date.
- Criteria written by the vendor and never agreed by the customer.
- One champion who uses the product while the rest of the team never logs in.
- No record of the outcome, so the team cannot learn from lost evaluations.

## Example

A working draft in GitLab's handbook separates trials from proofs of value. A trial gives access to the
product. A proof of value is a time-limited evaluation, agreed with the customer, that leads to a
decision. Every formal one should have a reason, named participants, a scope, measurable success
criteria, an owner, a decision date and a recorded result. The standard plan runs for 30 days.
