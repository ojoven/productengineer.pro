## Why it matters for a product engineer

A product engineer rarely owns the whole system. Platform, infrastructure, data and security
engineers own parts that every feature depends on. Their priorities are different: stability, cost,
risk. Working well with them means clear requests, changes that are easy to review, and respect for
their time and constraints. Reviewing their code with the same care builds the trust that makes the
next request easier.

## How to do it

1. Before asking another team for something, read their docs, and explain your goal, not only the change you want.
2. Keep changes small and self-contained, so they are easy to review.
3. Answer review requests quickly, without breaking your own focused work to do it.
4. In review, comment on design, behaviour and clarity, and explain why.
5. Pair on unfamiliar systems; it spreads knowledge faster than documents.

## Common mistakes

- Large changes that mix a refactor, a feature and a fix.
- Treating another team's review as a gate to get past.
- Review comments about personal taste, written as if they were rules.
- Changing another team's system without telling them.

## Example

Google publishes its code review guidelines. Reviewers should respond within one business day at
most, because slow reviews slow the whole team and put pressure on reviewers to accept weaker code.
But a developer in the middle of focused work should not stop to do a review. Authors should send
small changes: about 100 lines is usually reasonable, and 1,000 lines is usually too large. Small
changes are reviewed faster, are less likely to add bugs, and waste less work if they are rejected.
