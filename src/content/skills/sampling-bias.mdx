## Why it matters for a product engineer

Data comes from the people who were there to be measured. Survey respondents, power users and
customers who stayed are easy to study, and they are not typical. A product engineer who asks who is
missing from the data avoids building only for the most visible group.

## How to do it

1. Before the analysis, write down who could appear in the data, and who could not.
2. Compare the sample with your whole user base on a few known facts, such as plan, country and
   activity.
3. Look for survivorship. People who left, failed at signup or never installed are not in your user
   data.
4. When the sample is skewed, weight the results or report them per segment.
5. Recruit from more than one channel when you can.

## Common mistakes

- Believing that a big sample is a representative one.
- Studying only retained users to explain retention.
- Treating requests from power users as everyone's needs.
- Survey results reported without the response rate or the recruiting channel.

## Example

Stack Overflow's 2024 Developer Survey used 65,437 responses from 185 countries. Its methodology note
says that respondents were recruited mainly through Stack Overflow's own channels: messages on the
site, blog posts, the newsletter, banner ads and social media posts. So highly engaged users of the
site were more likely to see the invitation. The note states this bias openly, which lets readers
judge how far the results apply to developers who rarely visit the site.
