## Why it matters for a product engineer

People hesitate at decision points, such as choosing a plan, confirming a purchase or setting up an
account, and some of them leave. Engineers shape these moments directly: the default value, the
number of options, what is pre-selected and whether a confirmation step exists. The same tools can
remove friction from good choices and add it where a mistake is costly.

## How to do it

1. Find the decision points in a flow, and measure where people stop or go back.
2. Set a default that most people would choose, and make it easy to change.
3. Show fewer options, and recommend one when people cannot judge the difference.
4. Make options comparable: the same attributes, in the same order and the same units.
5. Add friction on purpose before actions that are hard to undo, such as deleting data or sending
   money.
6. Check that each default serves the user. If you could not explain it in public, it is a trick, not
   a default.

## Common mistakes

- Asking for a choice people cannot judge yet, such as a plan before they have used the product.
- Pre-selected extras that people must notice and remove.
- Removing every confirmation, including the one before a destructive action.
- Plan tables where each column lists different features.

## Example

From October 2012, UK employers had to enrol eligible employees in a workplace pension by default,
the largest employers first and all of them by 2018. People can still opt out, but they no longer have
to decide to join. In the private sector, participation went from 42% in 2012 to 86% in 2022. In
2022–23, fewer than 1% of savers made an active decision to stop.
