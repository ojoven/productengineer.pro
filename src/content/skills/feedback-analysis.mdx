## Why it matters for a product engineer

Support tickets, reviews and comments explain what the metrics only show. A drop in completion says
that something is wrong; ten tickets about the same error message say what. Engineers are often the
ones who can read a ticket and see the bug behind it, and with AI tools they can now read hundreds a
week instead of a handful.

## How to do it

1. Collect feedback in one place: support email, reviews, in-product comments and sales notes.
2. Read a sample yourself first. Write short codes for what each item is about.
3. Group the codes into themes. Name each theme as the user's problem, not as your solution.
4. Count themes over time. A theme that grows after a release is evidence; one loud email is not.
5. Use AI to code large volumes, but check its themes against the original messages.
6. Link each theme to data: who reported it, which plan they are on, and what they did in the
   product.

## Common mistakes

- Reacting to the loudest or most recent message.
- Themes that describe features ("wants export") instead of problems.
- Trusting AI-generated themes without reading the source text.
- Analysing feedback once, for a report, instead of every week.
- Forgetting that people who write are not everyone. See
  [sampling bias](/skills-map/measurement/statistical-reasoning/sampling-bias/).

## Example

Mamitsu turns social videos into data for apps and AI agents. Its feedback came through the support
email, and its maker read what people asked now and then, by hand. After a call for In practice, he set
up an agent that reads the week's requests every Monday. The first report arrived three days later.
([Mamitsu in In practice](/in-practice/mamitsu/))
