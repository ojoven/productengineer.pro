## Why it matters for a product engineer

The revenue model shapes the product and the code. A subscription needs value every month, usage
pricing needs reliable metering, a marketplace needs both sides to show up, and open source needs a
line between free and paid. Engineers who know the model build the right billing, limits and analytics
from the start, instead of adding them in a hurry later.

## How to do it

1. Write how the product earns money today, in one sentence: who pays, for what, and how often.
2. Check that revenue follows the value. When a customer gets more value, do they pay more?
3. Choose the model that fits how value arrives: once (licence), every month (subscription), with use
   (usage), with each deal (transaction fee).
4. List what the model needs in the product: plans and entitlements, a meter, invoices, a free tier,
   a commercial licence.
5. Plan how you will change it. Most products change their model at least once, so keep prices and
   rules out of the code where you can.
6. Watch the numbers the model depends on, such as churn for a subscription or usage per account.

## Common mistakes

- Copying the model of a famous company without its type of customer.
- A subscription for something people need once.
- Usage pricing without a meter customers can see and trust.
- An open-source project where the free version already covers what buyers would pay for.

## Example

Zernio changed its model twice in its first year. It launched with lifetime licences on AppSumo:
about 2,000 licences, a bit over $200,000 in sales, which paid for the first ads and the first hire.
Subscriptions came next, and usage-based billing in early 2026. Papermark is open core: a free AGPL
core anyone can self-host, a hosted service, and a commercial licence for the features in its `ee/`
folders. ([Zernio](/in-practice/zernio/), [Papermark](/in-practice/papermark/))
