## Why it matters for a product engineer

Some products need setup before they are useful: connecting data, inviting a team, choosing
settings. Guided setup splits that work into clear steps, shows progress and lets people come back
later. A product engineer decides what can have a default, what can wait, and what state to save
between sessions.

## How to do it

1. List the setup tasks, and remove or give a default to every one you can.
2. Group the rest into a few tasks with clear names, and show the status of each one.
3. Let people do the tasks in any order, unless one really depends on another.
4. Save progress after every task, so people can stop and return.
5. Keep visual customization, such as choosing a theme, out of the first setup. NN/g advises saving
   it for later.
6. Measure which task people stop at.

## Common mistakes

- One long form for all the setup.
- Forcing an order that the data does not need.
- Losing answers when someone leaves halfway.
- So many status labels that people cannot remember them.

## Example

GOV.UK services with long applications use a task list page. Each task shows a status, such as
"Completed" or "Incomplete", and "Cannot start yet" when it depends on another task. Users can
complete the tasks in any order. At the end of a task, they are asked whether they have completed it,
and they can answer "No, I'll come back to it later". The guidance says to start with as few
statuses as possible, because more statuses are harder to remember.
