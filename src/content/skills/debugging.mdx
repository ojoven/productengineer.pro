## Why it matters for a product engineer

Good debugging is a method: reproduce the problem, form a hypothesis, test it, and narrow down. It is
faster than guessing, and it finds the real cause instead of hiding it. A product engineer debugs
across the interface, the services and the data, and the same habit, evidence before fixes, works for
product problems too.

## How to do it

1. Reproduce the problem, and write down the exact steps. A bug you cannot reproduce is still a
   question.
2. Read the actual error message and the logs before you form a theory.
3. Ask what changed: a deploy, a dependency, a configuration, the data.
4. Form one hypothesis at a time, and design a test that could prove it wrong.
5. Narrow down: bisect the commits, the input or the configuration until one change explains it.
6. Understand the cause before you fix it. Then add a test, so it cannot return quietly.

## Common mistakes

- Changing several things at once, so you never learn which one mattered.
- Fixing the symptom, such as catching an exception, without knowing the cause.
- Trusting a report's explanation instead of its facts.
- Blaming the platform before checking your own code. Julia Evans finds the bug is her own "95% of the
  time".

## Example

In Trey Harris's well-known story, a university department could not send email further than about 500
miles. He reproduced it: Princeton, 400 miles, worked; Memphis, 600 miles, failed. A server update had
installed an older mail program that ignored his configuration and set the connection timeout to zero.
That aborts a connection in about three milliseconds, the time light needs to travel about 558 miles.
