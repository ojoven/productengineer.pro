## Why it matters for a product engineer

A product engineer makes dozens of decisions a day. Most are small: a name, a test, an edge case. A
few matter to other people: a data model, a public API, a price. Autonomy means making the small ones
yourself and knowing which big ones need others. That judgement comes from context: the goals, the
constraints, what the company has already tried. Autonomy grows as trust grows, and trust grows when
your decisions turn out well and you explain them.

## How to do it

1. Learn the goals and constraints well enough to explain them to someone else.
2. Decide alone when a decision is easy to undo and affects mainly your own work.
3. Involve others when it is hard to undo, costly, or affects other teams.
4. When you decide alone, write down what you decided and why.
5. Review your decisions later, and share what you learned.

## Common mistakes

- Asking permission for every small choice, which slows the whole team.
- Deciding alone on something that changes another team's work.
- Confusing autonomy with working without telling anyone.
- Managers who give autonomy without giving context.

## Example

Netflix describes its culture with the phrase "context not control". Managers are expected to give
their teams the context and clarity to make good decisions, instead of trying to control everything
themselves. For each significant decision, one "informed captain" makes the call, after seeking out
different opinions, and reflects on it afterwards so others can learn. Some policies are almost no
policy at all. The vacation policy is two words, and the expenses policy is five.
