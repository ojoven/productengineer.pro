## Why it matters for a product engineer

Every number from a sample is an estimate. A confidence interval shows how precise it is: the range
of values that fit the data. "22%, between 9% and 35%" says much more than "22%". It tells you when
to act, and when to wait for more data.

## How to do it

1. Report every rate or difference with its interval, not alone.
2. For a share, a quick 95% interval is the share plus or minus 1.96 × √(p × (1 − p) ÷ n).
3. For a difference between two groups, look at the interval of the difference. If it includes zero,
   the data cannot tell the groups apart yet.
4. Ask whether every value inside the interval would lead to the same decision. If yes, decide. If
   no, collect more data.
5. Remember that the interval only covers random noise, not a biased sample or a broken event.

## Common mistakes

- Reporting a percentage from a small sample with no interval.
- Reading "95% confidence" as "95% chance the true value is in this range" and then treating the
  middle as certain.
- Ignoring an interval that crosses zero because the middle looks good.
- Narrow intervals from biased data, trusted because they are narrow.

## Example

Superhuman's founder wrote that a product-market fit survey starts to give "directionally correct
results" at about 40 answers. Directional is the right word. If 22% of 40 people say they would be
very disappointed without the product, the 95% interval runs from about 9% to 35%. It takes about
1,000 answers to narrow it to a few points either way.
