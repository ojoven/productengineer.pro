## Why it matters for a product engineer

A channel that brings many cheap users who leave is expensive. Channel performance compares channels
on what their users do afterwards: activation, retention and revenue, not only volume and cost.
Engineers make this possible: the channel has to be recorded at sign-up and joined to product and
billing data, or every channel looks the same.

## How to do it

1. Record the channel and campaign at sign-up, and keep it on the account.
2. For each channel, measure volume, cost, activation, retention by cohort and revenue per account.
3. Compare channels by cost per retained or paying customer, not per sign-up.
4. Look at the kind of customer each channel brings. Two channels with the same cost can bring very
   different customers.
5. Move budget step by step, and check again. Results change as spend grows.

## Common mistakes

- Comparing channels by clicks or sign-ups.
- Judging a channel before its users are old enough to churn.
- One blended number for all channels.
- Losing the channel when a user signs up on another device.

## Example

About 40% of Zernio's new business comes from organic Google, and paid ads bring about a quarter of
new monthly revenue. Its founder cannot yet say the cost of a customer from one Google campaign, or the
churn of one segment. The two kinds of customer churn at about 2% and 17%, so a blended cost per
customer hides which clicks pay back. ([Zernio in In practice](/in-practice/zernio/))
