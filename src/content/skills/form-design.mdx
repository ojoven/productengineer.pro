## Why it matters for a product engineer

Forms are where people give a product information, and where many of them give up. Most form problems
are small: a missing label, the wrong keyboard, an error shown in the wrong place. An engineer who knows
the basics can fix most of them in an afternoon.

## How to do it

1. Ask only for what you need now. Every field must have a reason, and the user should see it.
2. Put labels above the fields, and keep them visible while people type. Do not use placeholder text
   as a label.
3. Use the right input type, so phones show the right keyboard, and support autofill.
4. Size each field to its expected answer. NN/g found that 99.9% of 2,130 city names fit in 19
   characters.
5. Validate after people finish a field, not while they type. Show the error next to the field, and
   keep what they typed.
6. Mark the optional fields, or better, remove them.

## Common mistakes

- Asking for everything at sign-up "because we might need it".
- Clearing the whole form after one error.
- One field for things people think of as separate, or several fields for one thing.
- Buttons that say "Submit" instead of what will happen.

## Example

Zernio gives two connected accounts free, with no card. It asks for a card only when someone connects
X, because X's API is paid. The form asks for payment at the moment the reason is
clear to the user. ([Zernio in In practice](/in-practice/zernio/))
