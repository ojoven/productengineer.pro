## Why it matters for a product engineer

A customer can like a product and still not use it fully. The team that bought it may need approval
from security, a connection to their existing tools, or a way to move old data. Inside the company,
some people may not want to change how they work. These barriers rarely show in product analytics.
Many of them are technical, so a product engineer can often remove them once someone finds them.

## How to do it

1. Talk to accounts with low usage, and ask what stops wider use: who must approve, what the product
   must connect to, what data must move.
2. Talk to the people who promote the product inside the company and to the people who block it, such
   as IT, security and finance.
3. List barriers by type, such as buying, security, network, integration, migration and habit, and
   count how many accounts each one blocks.
4. Remove the barriers that block the most growth first, often with admin controls, importers or
   integrations.
5. Tell the blocked accounts when a barrier is gone, and track whether their usage grows.

## Common mistakes

- Reading low usage as low interest, without asking.
- Hearing only from users, never from the people who approve.
- Building features for users while an admin need blocks the whole company.
- Removing a barrier and not telling the customers it blocked.

## Example

GitHub Copilot started as a tool for individual developers. By February 2023, more than 400
organizations already used it, and GitHub released Copilot for Business. Companies could buy licenses
online and assign seats, even if they did not keep their code on GitHub. Copilot gained support for
VPNs, including self-signed certificates. It also began to block common insecure suggestions, such as
hardcoded credentials and SQL injections.
