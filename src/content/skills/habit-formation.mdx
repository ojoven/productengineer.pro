## Why it matters for a product engineer

Products that people use often depend on habits: a trigger, an action, a reward and an investment
that repeat in a stable context. Engineers build most of these parts: the reminders, the speed of the
action and the saved state that makes the next visit better. The honest version helps people build a
habit they want, such as practising a language. The other version builds one they would not choose.

## How to do it

1. Start from a need that already recurs in people's lives. A product cannot make a rare need
   frequent.
2. Find the internal trigger: the feeling or situation that comes just before people would use the
   product.
3. Make the action the smallest step that gives value from that moment.
4. Make the reward the outcome people came for, and show it to them.
5. Ask for a small investment that improves the next visit, such as a saved preference, a reminder
   time or their own data.
6. Offer external triggers that people choose, such as a reminder at a time they set, and let them
   change or stop it easily.

## Common mistakes

- Notifications as the habit, with nothing behind them that people want.
- Rewards that keep people using a product that does not help them.
- No investment, so every visit starts from zero.
- Aiming for daily use when the need comes once a week.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp found a need that recurs: anxious people have many spikes a week. But a
free visitor who had a good session had no way to come back: no install prompt, no "send me this
link", no reminder and no share. Each visit was single use. A second review put a reminder, with its
own consent line, on the list of changes. ([RelievApp in In practice](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
