## Why it matters for a product engineer

A beta puts a new feature in front of engaged users before everyone sees it. They find the bugs, the
confusing parts and the missing pieces while change is still cheap. For a product engineer it is
also the first real usage data, and a direct line to the people who use the feature.

## How to do it

1. Plan the beta early, not when the feature is almost ready.
2. Write what success means first: the metrics and the questions the beta must answer.
3. Invite early adopters who will use it right away and accept rough edges. Give them some real
   value, or they will not come back.
4. Tell them what to expect: what works, what does not, and how long the beta lasts.
5. Make feedback easy, inside the product. Log each comment with its type and the reason behind it.
6. Set a date for the release decision, and decide from the metrics and the patterns in the
   feedback.

## Common mistakes

- A beta with no goal, so it never ends.
- Inviting only friendly users, who will not find the problems.
- Trying to build every request before the release.
- No reply to the people who gave feedback.

## Example

When Intercom took its new Respond Reports out of beta, there was "a mountain of feedback". Building
it all first would have delayed a better report for the 95% of customers who were not in the beta.
So the team released what it had, and kept the other requests for the next iterations. A date for
the decision worked as a forcing function.
