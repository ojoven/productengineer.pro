## Why it matters for a product engineer

A product engineer reads competitors to decide scope and quality, not to copy a feature list.
Knowing how others solve the same job shows what users will expect as normal, and where a small team
can be clearly better. It also stops you from spending a month on something a free tool already does
well.

## How to do it

1. Start from the job, not the category. List what people already use for it, including tools from
   other categories and [substitutes](/skills-map/discovery/market-research/substitutes/).
2. Sign up for the top two or three as a new user. Do the main task from start to finish, and note
   each step, the time it takes and where you get stuck.
3. Read what their users complain about: store reviews, review sites, forums, GitHub issues. Group
   the complaints and count how often each one appears.
4. Write down the price of each plan and what it unlocks. Price is part of the product.
5. Sort what you found into three groups: what you must match, where you can be clearly better, and
   what you will leave out on purpose.
6. Do it again every few months. Products change, and so do their prices.

## Common mistakes

- Copying the feature list of the market leader.
- Comparing only with products that look like yours.
- Reading the marketing pages instead of using the product.
- A comparison table where you win every row. Nobody believes it, and it hides what you should
  learn.
- Doing it once, before the first version, and never again.

## Example

Asked who else does what Mamitsu does, Unai Esteibar named Supadata and PostReef, with Apify as the
general version. Supadata's traction showed that people pay for the job, so his question was "Can I do
it as well or better, and much cheaper?". Mamitsu now publishes comparison pages against five rivals,
and the page against Supadata also shows where Supadata does more.
