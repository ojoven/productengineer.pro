## Why it matters for a product engineer

A launch concentrates attention on one day, and engineers carry most of the risk: the load, the bugs
and the support that arrive with new users. Choosing where to launch matters more than how loud the
launch is. And the days after count as much as the day itself, because that is when new users decide
whether to stay.

## How to do it

1. Choose the place where your buyers already are, with the intent to try something new: a
   marketplace, a community, a newsletter, a directory.
2. Write what people should do next, and make that path work before the day.
3. Prepare for the load and the support: monitoring, a way to roll back, and someone who answers.
4. Plan the follow-up: answers to questions, fixes in the first week, and a second moment later.
5. Consider a quiet launch first. Fix what the first users find, then launch more widely.
6. Measure the launch by users who stay a month later, not by votes or visits.

## Common mistakes

- Launching where your peers are, not your buyers.
- One big day with no plan for the next week.
- No capacity for support when the users arrive.
- Treating a quiet launch as a failure, instead of as a test.

## Example

Zernio's founder found that posts on LinkedIn "and the like" did little: "nobody cares, your ideal
customer is not there". He launched on AppSumo, which sends the offer to its own list of buyers.
Mamitsu chose a quiet launch first, with a wider relaunch next, Hacker News included.
