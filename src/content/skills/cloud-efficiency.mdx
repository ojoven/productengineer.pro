## Why it matters for a product engineer

Cloud bills grow quietly: a server sized for a launch that never shrinks, a test environment that
runs all weekend, data that nobody reads but everybody pays to keep. The people who can fix most of
it are the engineers who know how the product uses its resources. Finance sees the total. Engineers
see which feature, table or bucket causes it, and whether a cheaper option would still work for users.

## How to do it

1. Tag resources by team and feature, so each cost has an owner.
2. Measure use before you change anything: CPU, memory, requests, and how often data is read.
3. Remove what is idle, then resize what is too big, then commit to the capacity you always use.
4. Match storage to access: frequent data in fast storage, old data in cheaper classes.
5. Calculate the cost of the change itself, and how long it takes to pay back.

## Common mistakes

- Buying reserved capacity before removing waste, so you commit to the waste.
- Moving data to a cheaper class without checking how often it is read.
- Saving money once and never looking at the bill again.
- Complex setups that cost more in engineering time than they save.

## Example

Canva stores more than 230 petabytes in Amazon S3. Its analysis showed that people rarely open
designs and uploads again after the first weeks, so this data could move to a cheaper storage class.
Moving all 300 billion objects would have cost over $6 million, because each move has a price. Canva
started with buckets whose average object was 400 KB or more, where the saving paid back the move
within six months. It spent over $1.6 million to move about 80 billion objects. In 2023, the change saved
about $300,000 a month.
