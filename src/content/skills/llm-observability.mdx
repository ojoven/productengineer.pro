## Why it matters for a product engineer

An AI feature can fail without an error. The request succeeds, the response is fast, and the answer is
wrong, off topic, or worse than last week. Normal monitoring does not see this. You need a record of
each request: the prompt, the context, the model, the output, the tool calls, the tokens and the time.
With that record, a vague complaint becomes a case you can read, reproduce and add to your tests.

## How to do it

1. Record a trace for each AI request, with each model and tool call as a step.
2. Store the model version and prompt version with every trace.
3. Give users a simple way to flag a bad answer, and link the flag to the trace.
4. Read real traces every week, group the failures, and turn each group into test cases.
5. Remove or mask personal data before storage, and limit who can read traces.

## Common mistakes

- Logging only errors and latency, so wrong answers are invisible.
- Traces that nobody reads until there is an incident.
- No link between a trace and the deploy or config change that came before it.
- Storing full prompts with personal data that nobody is allowed to see.

## Example

In August 2025, three separate infrastructure bugs reduced the quality of some Claude responses. The
first started on 5 August and affected about 0.8% of Sonnet 4 requests. A routine load-balancing change
on 29 August made it worse: at the worst hour, 16% were affected. Anthropic's evaluations did not catch
the problem, and privacy controls limited what engineers could read. User reports were the signal.
Anthropic now runs quality evaluations continuously on production systems.
