## Why it matters for a product engineer

AI can now act: send messages, change data, ship code. Human oversight puts a person at the points
where a mistake would be expensive or hard to undo. It only works if the system shows that person
what the AI did and why, and if the system, not the model, enforces the decision. Product engineers
build those review points, and decide where they belong.

## How to do it

1. List the actions the AI can take, and rate each by the cost of a mistake and how easy it is to
   undo.
2. Let low-risk, reversible actions run alone, with a log.
3. Require approval for the rest: show the exact action, the evidence and the expected result.
4. Make approval, rejection and interruption work in the system, so a timeout means "no".
5. Keep the decisions that define the product with people: what to build, what to ship, for whom.
6. Review the log regularly, and move actions between levels as trust grows or falls.

## Common mistakes

- Approving everything, so review becomes a click without reading.
- Review screens that show the AI's summary, not the actual change.
- Oversight that the model can skip, because it is only an instruction in the prompt.
- The same level of control for every action, whatever the risk.

## Example

At RelievApp, agents built nine sessions, a mascot and a new front door in about 45 minutes. Its maker
kept the decisions that shape the product: the category, the catalog and what went to staging. When
an agent met a question only he could answer, it stopped and asked. Mamitsu's maker reviews only the
changes an agent scores as risky. ([RelievApp](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/),
[Mamitsu](/in-practice/mamitsu/))
