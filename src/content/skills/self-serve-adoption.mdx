## Why it matters for a product engineer

When people can sign up, try and pay without talking to anyone, each new customer costs almost
nothing to serve. That only works if the product explains itself. The work moves from sales calls to
the product: clear pricing, a first run that leads to value, and limits that show when it is time to
pay. Much of it is engineering.

## How to do it

1. Let people start without a card or a call: a free plan or a trial, with a short sign-up.
2. Publish the prices. A hidden price sends people to a sales call or away.
3. Make the first run lead to value without help.
4. Put the paid limits where people meet them naturally: more seats, more usage, a feature a growing
   team needs.
5. Let people pay in the product, and change or cancel their plan there too.
6. Add sales only where it pays, for example for accounts above a size, and say where that line is.

## Common mistakes

- "Book a demo" as the only way in.
- A free plan so generous that nobody needs to pay, or so limited that nobody sees the value.
- Self-serve sign-up, and then an onboarding that needs a person.
- Prices that change from one page to the next.

## Example

Papermark has a free plan with no card. About 100 people sign up each day, and "others just stay on
the free version forever and that's totally fine". Zernio lets developers connect two accounts free,
with no card and no sales call; sales only joins past 2,000 accounts.
