## Why it matters for a product engineer

People use the same product on a phone, a tablet and a laptop, with touch, a mouse or a keyboard.
Responsive design adapts the layout and the interaction to each. It is more than making things
smaller: on a small screen the order of content and the size of targets change too. Almost all of it
is code.

## How to do it

1. Add the viewport meta tag. Without it, a phone renders the page at a desktop width, often about
   980 px, and shrinks it.
2. Start from the smallest screen and add layout as space allows.
3. Let the content choose the breakpoints: add one where the layout starts to look wrong, not at the
   width of a popular device.
4. Size things in relative units, and let images and media scale with their container.
5. Make touch targets large enough for a thumb, and do not depend on hover.
6. Test on real devices, at real widths such as 390 px, in both orientations.

## Common mistakes

- Designing for desktop and squeezing it onto a phone.
- Hiding content on small screens that people need there.
- Breakpoints for specific devices, which break on the next device.
- Text or controls that only work with a mouse.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp asked for every scene to be checked at 390 px in each look. The
minimal look needed its own contrast pass: white text over the pale sphere was hard to read, and
"Drag up to breathe in" almost disappeared. A layout that works at one width and in one theme is not
yet done. ([RelievApp in In practice](/in-practice/relievapp-part-1/))
