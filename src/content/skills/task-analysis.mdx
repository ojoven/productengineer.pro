## Why it matters for a product engineer

Task analysis breaks a goal into the steps and decisions people take to reach it. It shows which steps
the goal really needs and which exist only because of today's tools. Engineers write the code for
each step, so they can often remove a step that only the old tool needed.

## How to do it

1. Separate the goal from the tasks. Nobody's goal is to fill in a form; the form is a task on the way
   to something they want.
2. Watch people do the work, or talk to experts who do it, and write each step and each decision.
3. Note what triggers each step, what information it needs and what can go wrong.
4. Group the steps into a hierarchy: the goal, its tasks, and the steps inside each task.
5. Mark each step as needed by the goal or needed by the tool.
6. Design to remove the tool's steps first, and to support the goal's steps.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing goals with tasks, and optimising a task nobody wants.
- Writing the steps from the documentation instead of from what people do.
- Ignoring the decisions between steps, where people hesitate.
- Analysing one person and treating it as everyone.

## Example

Before Mamitsu, its maker built a coffee app from a daily routine. Each new bag starts from a base
recipe and changes a little each day: a bit finer, a bit more water. The hard part was not the recipe
but the grinders: one goes by numbers, another by clicks. "It looks easy. It is a mess." So the app
translated a recipe for one grinder to yours. ([Mamitsu in In practice](/in-practice/mamitsu/))
