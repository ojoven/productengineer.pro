## Why it matters for a product engineer

Privacy law is not only a page of legal text. Many of its duties are product features: an export of
someone's data, a way to correct it, a deletion that really deletes. Under the GDPR, a company must
answer such a request within one month. It must report a serious data breach to the authority within
72 hours. A product engineer knows where personal data lives, so they can tell whether these duties
are possible at all. A data model without an owner for each field makes every request slow and risky.

## How to do it

1. Keep a list of the personal data you store: which fields, where, why, and for how long.
2. Collect only the data a feature needs, and set a deletion rule for each kind.
3. Build export and deletion as features, with tests, before the first request arrives.
4. Explain each use of data in plain words, close to the data itself.
5. Write down who decides and who acts when a breach happens, with the 72-hour limit in mind.

## Common mistakes

- Copies in logs, backups and analytics tools that a deletion does not reach.
- Exports that are complete but impossible for a person to understand.
- Keeping data "in case it is useful later".
- Leaving privacy to the legal team until a request or a complaint arrives.

## Example

In 2023, the Swedish privacy authority audited how Spotify answered access requests. Spotify did
release the data. It even split it into layers: one with the data most people want, such as payment
details and listening history, and one with technical log files. The authority said the layers were
fine. The problem was the explanation: Spotify did not say clearly enough how and why it used the data.
The authority fined Spotify SEK 58 million.
