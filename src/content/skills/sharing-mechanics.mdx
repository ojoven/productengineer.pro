## Why it matters for a product engineer

The cheapest growth comes from what users already share: a report, a design, a result, a document.
When the shared thing is useful to the person who receives it and shows where it came from, every
use of the product reaches someone new. Engineers decide most of this: the link, the preview, what
the visitor sees without an account, and the path from there to the product.

## How to do it

1. Find what users already send to others, and make it a first-class object: a page with its own
   link, not an attachment.
2. Make the shared page work for the visitor without sign-up. They came for the content, not the
   product.
3. Show where it was made, quietly: the domain, a small mark, a link to try it.
4. Design the preview: title, description and image, so the link looks good in chat apps and social
   networks.
5. Think about what the link reveals. A shared link can be forwarded to anyone.
6. Measure visitors per shared item and how many of them start using the product.

## Common mistakes

- Branding so loud that people stop sharing.
- Shared pages that ask the visitor to sign up first.
- Links with no preview, which look like spam in a chat.
- Links that reveal private details about the person who shared them.

## Example

Every Papermark link without a custom domain lives on papermark.com, so every investor or buyer who
opens a data room sees the product. Many of them run deals too. RelievApp's gift link names the
session, never the feeling, and opens free with the sender's name.
