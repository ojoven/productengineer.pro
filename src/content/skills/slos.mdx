## Why it matters for a product engineer

A service level objective (SLO) is a reliability target that the team agrees on, such as "99.9% of
requests succeed over 30 days". The error budget is what is left: 0.1% may fail. It turns the endless
argument between speed and stability into a number. While budget remains, the team ships. When it
runs out, reliability work comes first.

## How to do it

1. Choose indicators that match what users feel: successful requests, fast responses, jobs finished
   on time.
2. Measure them as close to the user as you can, for example at the load balancer, not inside one
   server.
3. Set a target lower than 100%. Users cannot tell 99.99% from 100%, and it costs much more.
4. Agree in writing what happens when the budget runs out, such as a pause on risky releases.
5. Alert on how fast the budget is being used, not on every error.
6. Review the targets every few months against complaints and real use.

## Common mistakes

- A 100% target, which leaves no room for any release.
- Indicators from inside the system that users never feel, such as CPU use.
- Targets set without the product team, so nobody acts when they are missed.
- An error budget that never changes a decision.

## Example

Zernio publishes its production numbers, failures included. In the 30 days to 23 November 2026, 97.1%
of 5.2 million posts were published. The page says why it shows failures: "APIs break. Tokens
expire. Platforms return 500s at 2am." An SLO for such a product has to separate failures the team controls
from failures on the platforms. ([Zernio in In practice](/in-practice/zernio/))
