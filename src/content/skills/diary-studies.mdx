## Why it matters for a product engineer

Many products are used in short moments spread over weeks: a learning app, a tool in a weekly routine.
A single session sees one of those moments, and memory changes the rest. A diary study records each
moment close to when it happens. It shows habits, repeats, and the days the product was not used at
all.

## How to do it

1. Choose one question about behaviour over time, and a period that covers several cycles of it: days
   for a daily habit, weeks for a weekly one.
2. Make each entry very cheap, in a tool people already carry: a text message, a photo, a short form
   on the phone.
3. Capture a short note in the moment. Ask the full questions later the same day.
4. Send reminders, and talk to each person at the start, in the middle and at the end.
5. Pay people for taking part, not for each entry, so nobody invents entries.
6. Read the entries while the study runs, and ask about unclear ones before people forget.

## Common mistakes

- Entries that take minutes to write, so people stop after three days.
- A period too short to show a pattern.
- Reading the diaries only at the end, when it is too late to ask.
- Forgetting that people skip moments they think are not important enough to report.

## Example

In a 2008 study, 20 people logged every information need they had while away from home or work, for
two weeks. Each person sent a short text message in the moment, answered six questions on a website
each evening, and had three interviews seven days apart. They were paid for the evening visits and the
interviews, not per entry. The diaries showed what people did when they could not look something up:
they called someone, waited, or let it go.
