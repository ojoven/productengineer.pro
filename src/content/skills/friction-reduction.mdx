## Why it matters for a product engineer

Users rarely leave because of one big problem. They leave after many small ones: a slow page, a
setting with a confusing name, a step they repeat every day. Each one seems too small to plan, so it
never reaches the roadmap. A product engineer can often fix one in an hour, and a steady habit of
fixing them changes how the whole product feels.

## How to do it

1. Collect small annoyances in one list, from support, feedback, session replays and your own use.
2. Define what counts: easy to fix, but noticed by many users. Larger problems go to the normal
   roadmap.
3. Reserve fixed time for them, such as a weekly slot or a rotation, so they do not wait behind
   features.
4. Group them by area, so one person can fix several in the same code.
5. Tell users what was fixed, and track the count over time.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until there are enough to justify a project.
- Letting large features enter the list as small fixes.
- Fixing only what engineers notice, not what users report.
- Fixes that wait weeks for review, so the habit stops.

## Example

In 2009, Ubuntu started One Hundred Paper Cuts: a project to fix 100 small usability bugs, easy to fix
but annoying, before the October release. The team fixed 76 tracked paper cuts in Ubuntu 9.10. They
believed the real number was closer to 100, because many fixes were never recorded. Fifteen fixes had
patches that were never merged, so the next cycle started with them. That cycle was split into ten
rounds of about a week, several of them on one application, and fixed 102.
