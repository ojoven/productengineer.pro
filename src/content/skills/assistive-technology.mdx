## Why it matters for a product engineer

Screen readers, magnifiers and voice control read and operate the interface for the people who use
them. Automated checkers find only part of the problems. Ten minutes with a screen reader shows things
no checklist catches: an unnamed button, a confusing order, an update nobody announces. Engineers can
do this basic pass themselves.

## How to do it

1. Learn one screen reader on a device you have. VoiceOver is built into Macs and iPhones; NVDA is
   free on Windows; TalkBack is built into Android.
2. Learn a few commands: move to the next item, next heading, next link and next form field.
3. Do a key task with the screen off or your eyes closed: sign up, search, buy.
4. Listen for what is missing: names on buttons and fields, headings, the result of an action.
5. Try zoom at 200% and the operating system's text size settings.
6. Run an automated checker too, but treat it as the start, not the proof.

## Common mistakes

- Testing only with automated tools.
- Testing with a screen reader only once, at the end of a project.
- Fixing what the checker reports and calling the page accessible.
- Dynamic updates, such as "Saved" or an error, that a screen reader never announces.

## Example

WebAIM's 2026 scan found that a third of form inputs on the top million home pages had no proper label.
An automated checker can catch a missing label. It cannot tell you that the label is "Field 1", or that
the error appears where nobody hears it. That takes a person with a screen reader.
