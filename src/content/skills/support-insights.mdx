## Why it matters for a product engineer

Support tickets show, every day and in users' own words, where people get stuck. Analytics shows where
people drop off, and tickets often say why. When engineers read tickets, they see bugs the way users
experience them, and the fix is often quick. Patterns across many tickets show where a larger
investment would pay back.

## How to do it

1. Send tickets to the team that owns the code, so the people who can fix a problem see it.
2. Tag each ticket with area, type (bug, question, request, confusing design) and customer segment.
3. End each ticket with a product action when you can: a fix, an issue the user can follow, or a
   docs change.
4. Review tag trends every week or month, and rank them by volume and by risk of losing customers.
5. Check that each fix reduced the tickets it was meant to reduce.

## Common mistakes

- A separate support team whose learning never reaches engineering.
- Closing a ticket with an answer, while the confusing screen stays the same.
- Free-text reasons that nobody can count.
- Judging trends by the loudest customer, not by volume.

## Example

At PostHog, one engineer in each engineering team is the Support Hero for a week. Support engineers
first triage tickets for most teams, and escalate what they cannot solve. The Support Hero then works
through the team's list. They fix bugs and confusing designs, build small features that suit the
target users, or open an issue and send the user the link. When a feature already exists but the user
could not find it, they link the docs or make the docs clearer.
