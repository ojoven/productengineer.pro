## Why it matters for a product engineer

Support sees every rough edge of the product, every day. Each ticket is a signal about a bug, a
confusing screen or a missing feature. When engineers work closely with support, problems reach the
right person faster and customers get real answers. Engineers also learn how the product is used,
which is hard to learn any other way. But support work interrupts deep work, so the way engineers take
part needs a clear structure.

## How to do it

1. Agree how support escalates to engineering: what to include, where it goes, how fast to expect an answer.
2. Put engineers on a support rotation, with less feature work during their week.
3. Build tools that help support answer alone: admin views, clear error messages, logs they can read.
4. Close the loop: tell support when a fix ships, so they can tell the customer.
5. Review patterns in tickets regularly, and turn the biggest ones into product work.

## Common mistakes

- Bug reports that go into a backlog and never come back.
- Every engineer watching every channel, so nobody can focus.
- Engineers on rotation who answer tickets but never report the patterns.
- Support tools built last, or never.

## Example

In February 2026, Inngest, a company that makes developer tools, described its support rotation. As it
grew, every engineer watched Slack and Discord for support questions, and nobody fully owned support.
Daily rotations did not help: no one ever fully left support mode, and tickets went to people outside
their area. The team moved to two tiers. A support engineer answers simple questions and prepares
context for the hard ones. Two engineers, one from each technical team, rotate weekly with fewer
feature commitments, and end the week with a recap of patterns.
