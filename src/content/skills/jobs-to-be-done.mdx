## Why it matters for a product engineer

Jobs to be done explains why people choose a product. With it, an engineer can judge a feature by the
progress it gives someone, not by who asked for it. It also shows the real competition: everything
else a person could use for the same job.

## How to do it

1. Talk to people who recently switched to your product or away from it. The job is clearest at the
   moment of the switch.
2. Rebuild their timeline: what happened first, what made them look, what they tried and what made
   them decide.
3. Write each job as a situation, a motivation and an outcome: "When …, I want to …, so I can …".
4. Note what they used before, even when it was not a product.
5. Group the jobs, and rank them by how often they come up and how badly they are served today.
6. Check each feature against one job. If no job needs it, question it.

## Common mistakes

- Jobs about your product ("I want to use the dashboard") instead of the person's progress.
- Jobs so broad that they fit any product, such as "be more productive".
- Treating a demographic group as a job.
- Finding the main job and then leaving it in a research document, not in the product and its copy.

## Example

An agent's audit of RelievApp listed seven jobs. The strongest was not "calm me down" but "a way in
that isn't meditation", for people who know breathing helps but cannot sit still with it. It was its
maker's own story, and the product said it only in a FAQ.
