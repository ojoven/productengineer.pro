## Why it matters for a product engineer

Many products can do more than their users know. People learn the first workflow, then stop
exploring. Education, such as guides, courses and worked examples, shows them the next workflow and
why it matters. For complex products, it can also teach a way of working that the product supports,
which brings in new customers too. Product engineers know how the features really work, so they can
write accurate examples and keep the material close to the task.

## How to do it

1. Find the features and workflows that retained customers use and other customers do not.
2. Teach the job, not the interface: start from a goal, then show the steps in the product.
3. Put short help where the task happens, and link to longer guides for people who want more.
4. Update the material when the product changes, as part of the release.
5. Measure whether people who use the material adopt the workflow and stay longer.

## Common mistakes

- Courses that cover every feature, with no clear goal.
- Screenshots that no longer match the product.
- Education on a separate site that nobody finds from inside the product.
- Counting views of the material, not changes in what customers do.

## Example

Basecamp published "Shape Up", an online book about how its team builds products. One chapter explains
hill charts. Each piece of work first goes uphill, while the team works out its approach, then
downhill, while they do the work. Basecamp built hill charts into its product, where teams drag each
piece of work into position with a few clicks. The book links to a guide on how to use the method in
Basecamp.
