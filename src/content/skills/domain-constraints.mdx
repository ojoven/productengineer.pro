## Why it matters for a product engineer

Constraints decide what can be adopted, not only what can be built. A solution that ignores an
approval process, a platform rule or a legacy system looks great in a demo and fails in real use.
Engineers who learn the constraints early avoid redesigns, and sometimes find the product itself
inside them.

## How to do it

1. List the constraints of the domain: laws, platform rules, budgets, approval chains, legacy
   systems, seasons, safety.
2. Ask people who work in the domain what slows every change down. Read the platform documentation
   and terms yourself.
3. Mark each one as hard (a law, a platform policy) or soft (a habit, a preference).
4. Design around the hard ones from the start. Test the slowest one early, for example by sending an
   app for review in the first week.
5. Write them down where the team sees them, with the date and the source.
6. Check them again when a platform or a law changes.

## Common mistakes

- Finding out about an approval process in launch week.
- Treating a habit as a rule, and so missing a simpler design.
- Building on a rule a platform tolerates today and could change tomorrow.
- Forgetting the buyer's side: procurement, security reviews, budget cycles.

## Example

Zernio's product sits inside the platforms' rules. Meta asks for a screencast and about 14 days of
review for each permission. YouTube limits uploads until it raises your quota. WhatsApp charges for
template messages outside a 24-hour window. Each developer who connects their users' accounts meets
these limits. Zernio meets them once, for every customer, and sells that work as an API.
