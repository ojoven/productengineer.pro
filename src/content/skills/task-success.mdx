## Why it matters for a product engineer

Task success asks the most direct question about usability: did people do what they came to do? It
measures completion, time and errors on the tasks that matter. A product engineer can measure it in
analytics at scale, and in usability tests in depth, and use both before and after a change.

## How to do it

1. Choose a few key tasks, each with a clear start and a clear end.
2. Measure three things: the share of attempts that complete, the time they take, and the errors on
   the way.
3. Decide how to score partial success, such as an order placed with a wrong detail, and keep that
   rule.
4. In analytics, define completion from events: the start event and the end event within a time
   window.
5. In usability tests, watch where people fail, because analytics shows that they failed but not
   why.
6. Compare versions with an A/B test, using the same tasks and the same definitions.

## Common mistakes

- Measuring time on page as if longer meant better.
- Only counting complete success, so a task that nearly works looks like a failure.
- Percentages from five test participants, without a range.
- Tasks chosen by the team, not by what people come to do.

## Example

Google Maps once had two search boxes: a dual box for local search, "what" and "where" apart, and a
single box for everything. The team believed the single box was simpler. In an A/B test, one group
saw only the single box, and the team compared error rates between the groups. People with the
single box adapted their searches successfully, so the dual box was removed for everyone.
