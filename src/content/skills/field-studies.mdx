## Why it matters for a product engineer

An interview tells you what people remember. Observation shows what they do, including the workarounds
they no longer see as problems. One visit can show an engineer the real data, the other tools open at
the same time and the step that takes ten minutes, which no ticket describes.

## How to do it

1. Agree on the research questions first, and on what you want to see.
2. Visit people where the work happens. GOV.UK suggests visits of one to three hours, and no more than
   four one-hour visits in a day, because observation is tiring.
3. Go in pairs. One person leads the visit; the other takes notes, photos and recordings.
4. Choose how much to interrupt before you go. Every question changes the work you are watching.
5. At the end, ask about anything you did not understand. Ask for consent again before you take new
   photos.
6. Analyse with your team soon after, and keep quotes and photos to show what you saw.

## Common mistakes

- Running the visit like an interview, so the work never happens.
- Watching only the end user and ignoring the people around the work, such as support staff.
- Going alone with poor notes, so the findings stay in one head.
- Fixing the workaround you saw without asking why people need it.

## Example

A DWP team built a digital way to send the DS1500, the form a doctor or nurse fills in so that a
terminally ill person can claim a benefit fast. By post, the form took 10 days on average, and often
longer. The researchers visited hospitals and surgeries to learn how doctors and Macmillan nurses work,
because they, not the patients, fill in the form. In private beta, the forms arrived in seconds.
