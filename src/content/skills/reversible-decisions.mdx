## Why it matters for a product engineer

Jeff Bezos calls them one-way and two-way doors. Through a two-way door you can walk back, so you can
decide fast and learn from the result. A one-way door, such as a public API, a data model in
production or a price promise to customers, is hard or impossible to undo, and deserves slow, careful
thought. Teams get both wrong: they debate small choices for weeks, and rush the few that will stay
for years. Product engineers can often turn a one-way door into a two-way door with a feature flag,
a staging environment or a migration path.

## How to do it

1. Before a decision, ask: if this is wrong, what does it cost to undo, and who is affected?
2. Decide cheap, reversible choices quickly, and let one person own them.
3. For hard-to-reverse choices, write the options down, ask for review and take your time.
4. Make more decisions reversible: flags, staged rollouts, versioned APIs, data you can migrate.
5. Check from time to time whether a reversible change has quietly become permanent.

## Common mistakes

- Treating every decision as a one-way door, so the team moves slowly everywhere.
- Treating a public API or pricing change as easy to reverse.
- Changing direction so often that moving is confused with learning.
- Reversible experiments that are never removed or decided.
