## Why it matters for a product engineer

Customer acquisition cost (CAC) is what you spend to win one paying customer. Next to lifetime value
and payback time, it shows whether growth makes money or burns it. Product engineers change it
directly: a better trial, a referral flow or a free tool lowers the cost of every customer, and
tracking that tells you which channel each customer came from.

## How to do it

1. Add up sales and marketing spend for a period, including salaries and tools, and divide by the new
   paying customers in that period.
2. Calculate it per channel as well as blended. A cheap organic channel can hide an expensive paid one.
3. Calculate payback: months of gross margin from a customer needed to cover their CAC.
4. Compare CAC with lifetime value. A common target is lifetime value at least three times CAC.
5. Recalculate as you spend more. The next customer from a channel usually costs more than the last.

## Common mistakes

- Only counting ad spend and leaving out salaries.
- A blended CAC that makes paid channels look cheap.
- Using signups instead of paying customers.
- Comparing CAC with revenue instead of gross margin.

## Example

Zernio's blended cost per paying customer is about €20, because about 40% of new business comes from
organic search. Paid ads alone take ten months to pay back. At €49.50 a year, RelievApp could not pay
for each customer with ads, so its distribution has to be part of the product.
