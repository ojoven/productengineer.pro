## Why it matters for a product engineer

A roadmap tells the team, and often customers, what comes next and why. Engineers use it to plan
architecture: what to make flexible now because it will change soon. A roadmap made of fixed features
and dates breaks the first time you learn something, so the format matters as much as the content.

## How to do it

1. Start from goals and problems, not features. Each item says what it should change for users.
2. Group items by confidence: now (being built), next (understood), later (ideas). Dates only where a
   real deadline exists.
3. Say why each item is there: the goal it serves, the evidence behind it.
4. Write the rule for what gets in. For example: build what many customers would use, not what one
   asks for.
5. Share it, and review it often, such as every two weeks for "now" and every quarter for "later".
6. Keep a list of what you said no to, with the reason.

## Common mistakes

- A list of features with dates, treated as a promise.
- So many items in "now" that nothing is finished.
- Adding every customer request.
- A roadmap nobody outside the team can read.
- Never removing items from "later".

## Example

Zernio's integrators often ask for the next channel. The rule is to build it when the rest of the
users would use it too. In 2026 that added ads, WhatsApp, phone numbers and commerce APIs, often after
integrators asked. Ads are about 15% of accounts and get the most requests. Each new product bills
itself through the same price per account, with no new checkout.
