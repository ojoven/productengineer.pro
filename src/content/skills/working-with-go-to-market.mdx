## Why it matters for a product engineer

Marketing and sales talk to more potential customers than anyone else. They hear what buyers need,
what competitors promise and why deals fail. Engineers who help them get that knowledge in return.
Engineers can help with demos, hard technical questions and fast fixes for problems that block a deal.
They also protect the company by checking that public claims about the product are true. A claim that
the product cannot keep is a technical problem, a trust problem and sometimes a legal one.

## How to do it

1. Join some sales calls or demos, and listen to how buyers describe their problem.
2. Review the technical claims in marketing and sales material before it is published.
3. Give go-to-market teams simple, accurate wording for security, performance and limits.
4. Agree a fast path for problems that block a deal, and a rule for requests too specific to build.
5. Tell them early about launches, changes and known problems.

## Common mistakes

- Building every feature that a single prospect asks for.
- Leaving technical claims to people who cannot check them.
- Treating sales questions as interruptions instead of market data.
- Launching features before sales and support know how they work.

## Example

In November 2020, Zoom settled with the US Federal Trade Commission over its security claims. The FTC
alleged that since at least June 2016, Zoom had said that meetings used end-to-end, 256-bit
encryption. In fact, Zoom's servers held the keys, and meetings used 128-bit keys. Zoom also said
cloud recordings went to its secure storage once a meeting ended; some waited on its servers,
unencrypted, for up to 60 days. An engineer who knew the product could have checked each claim.
