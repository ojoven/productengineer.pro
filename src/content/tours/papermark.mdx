<Stop skill="competitive-analysis">
"Everyone knew DocSend and everyone didn't like it." In Marc's telling, it stopped improving after Dropbox bought it, and later dropped its free plan.

<Hot>The best incumbent to copy is one people already pay for and complain about: the market is proven and the complaints are your roadmap.</Hot>
</Stop>

<Stop skill="market-trends">
In 2023 open-source alternatives to older SaaS were a wave: Cal.com for Calendly, Documenso for DocuSign. Papermark was early to do the same for DocSend, and its OSS Friends page still lists the others.

<Hot>Marc's own view is that the timing was luck. The choice of incumbent was not: he picked the one nobody else in that wave had taken.</Hot>
</Stop>

<Stop skill="willingness-to-pay">
"The first customers started saying: can we pay you money?" Iuliia said they paid even though the payment flow was not finished.

<Hot>When people ask to pay before you have a checkout, the price is not your problem yet. Finish the checkout.</Hot>
</Stop>

<Stop skill="competitive-advantage">
Marc's four reasons, on Starter Story: nobody can charge less than a free core, anyone can try it, the community adds R&D, and a bank can audit the code instead of asking for access.

<Hot>The audit point is the one that pays. Deal teams buy trust, and code anyone can read is trust they can check.</Hot>
</Stop>

<Stop skill="differentiation">

<Hot>It also hands control to the customer: with a custom domain, the papermark.com loop stops. They give up some viral reach on the plans that pay most.</Hot>
</Stop>

<Stop skill="async-systems">
PDF-to-image conversion, video optimisation, bulk downloads, visit exports and notifications run as Trigger.dev jobs in `lib/trigger`, outside the request.

<Hot>For a document viewer this is most of the backend: the slow work is converting files, and none of it should block a visitor.</Hot>

<Stop skill="data-protection">
Dynamic watermarks with the viewer's details, screenshot protection, NDAs before access, email verification, allow and block lists, and one-time codes for bulk downloads.

<Hot>Every one of these is a sentence an M&A lawyer can tick on a checklist. Here, protection is what the product sells.</Hot>
</Stop>
